United thumped and stumped in Como.

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · Friday 23 October 2026

Manchester United were defeated convincingly 4-1 by a well coached and well motivated Como on Wednesday and, minutes after, Michael Carrick and Bruno Fernandes had both had their say on the record as to why. United were a goal down within two minutes, which created visible doubt in the reds side. There was a real confusion that set in early and snowballed into the worst defeat under Michael Carrick. Bruno Fernandes was clearly disappointed, not only for the loss but for being substituted in the 67th minute. Most of the pundits since have weighed in on this potentially sackable defeat and run of results for Carrick. Some pundits have picked a side, some blast the team, some the manager and some both plus the ownership. Not all of them are arguing about the same thing and no-one seems to fully agree on where to park how much of the blame. Lack of answers hasn’t stopped many having their say on what or who could turn things around for United as the club spirals further into crisis.

After a seven-match winless run and a week of reports about who might replace Carrick, we’ve gathered a list of just some of the outlets and managerial candidates named. We’ve tried to categorise these claims based on merit and the types of reporting.

The United Roundup view

Opinion.

Overview

The player/manager rift has been handy for anyone who would rather not examine the deeper issues at Manchester United. Beyond the Glazers leverage buy-out of the club and Sir Jim Ratcliffe, an owner that can only spend 90 days a year in the UK, the Man City connections of Omar Berrada and Jason Wilcox and a fanbase that is angry and tired in equal measure, United are underperforming consistently on the pitch. On Wednesday they were a side with nine players out, seven teenagers on the bench and a centre-back on his first appearance in 325 days was always going to look a long way from a pressing team, whoever drew up the plan.

The Game

United lost heavily. After we went a goal down, it never really looked as if we were going to get hold of the game. Supporters are entitled to ask what the tactical plan was. Carrick’s “half-pressing” comment for me is hard to swallow and Bruno’s actions in the game saw him actively attempt to press at times but ultimately wasn’t backed up by his team mates. Then his comments on top of that tell me he wasn’t happy with the instructions and tried to take things into his own hands during the game.

Interestingly, Roy Keane made a comment on an episode of the Stick to Football podcast where Ian Wright was defending Bruno and Keane was having none of it, he went on to explain, angrily, that he sees players (Bruno included) simulating pressing or pretending to press, where they would run towards an opposition player but not get to him in time, then back off as if to say “well, I’ve done my bit”. I’m inclined to agree with Keane on this. There seemed to be a lot of that going on against Como. And in previous games this season.

On the flip-side, there were moments where individuals did pressure the ball but there was a clear disconnect among the United players which meant that when they did, there was always a Como player in acres of space to hand it off to, and that’s not to take away from Como, their movement with and without the ball was far superior to United’s. They exposed us in all areas of the pitch and passed the ball around with ease compared to United, who could barely keep hold of the ball, let alone string passes together. Whether Carrick’s men were asked to press but didn’t know when or they had clear instructions but were out-played is up for debate. How much of the confusion and desperation came from conceding so early…again, and how much was poor coaching and tactical preparation we can only speculate. But once it set in, it spiralled out of control and United never looked like they were going to beat the home side, who had the bit between their teeth. Como saw blood and went for the kill.

After taking this absolute mauling, the coach and the captain gave different reasons and excuses as to why, both on the record. Nobody has shown that they can’t both be partly correct, mind. Whether you feel that Bruno was being selfish and throwing the manager under the bus, or whether you think he was just venting his frustration after an embarrassing and abysmal personal and team performance - most agree that, similar to the Martinez outburst in Madrid, that a post match interview is not the time or place to question the coaching staff and managers’ instructions. Fernandes saying his piece in public and then Carrick answering publicly made it a two-way argument.

What can’t be claimed as fact is that Carrick has lost the dressing room, but we can assume that there would’ve been some robust conversations going on in the debriefs and over the following days. But United have another game on Sunday so they can’t afford to dwell on it for too long. My hope is that there are clear plans and constructive training sessions this week. What else isn’t certain is whether the club has chosen a replacement or how under way the arrangements for Carrick’s successor are. Five outlets have named candidates this week, none with a named source, and they disagree on who United have approached, who wants whom and whether the most-mentioned names can be had at all. Bournemouth on Sunday won’t settle any of it, but a visible pressing plan would tell us more than another round of quotes.
