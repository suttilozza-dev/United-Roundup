WHO’S SAYING WHAT

Lammens answers his critics, and a scholarship offer lands for United’s most contested 16-year-old

Paul Scholes doubts whether Lammens is title-winning quality. Roy Keane has a separate complaint entirely. Lammens has answered one and not the other. Meanwhile United are trying to keep JJ Gabriel — and The Guardian already thinks they’ll fail.

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · Friday 9 October 2026

Two separate arguments about United did the rounds this week, and neither has much to do with Saturday’s Tottenham game — though both will get read through it anyway. The first is whether Senne Lammens is actually good enough to win United the league, after Paul Scholes and Roy Keane each had a go at him for completely different reasons. The second is whether United can hang on to a 16-year-old academy forward at all, now there’s a formal offer on the table and a national newspaper’s already called the odds against them. Different stakes, different sourcing, but both are proper, evidenced disagreements rather than manufactured ones — so both get the full treatment below.

Part one: Is Senne Lammens good enough to win United the league?

Senne Lammens has had two different critics this autumn asking two different questions. Paul Scholes wants to know whether he is title-winning quality. Roy Keane wants to know why he was smiling for photos with family on the pitch straight after United lost the derby. Lammens has answered the first one directly. The second, as far as anyone can find on the record, he hasn’t addressed at all.

What has been confirmed

United lost 1–0 at home to Manchester City in the derby inside their first five Premier League games this season, the only goal an Erling Haaland strike. Former referee Mark Clattenburg argued at the time that the goal shouldn’t have stood, on the grounds that Enzo Fernández was offside and interfering with play (Confirmed, via United In Focus, 13 September).

Lammens joined United from Royal Antwerp for £18.1m in summer 2025 and was first-choice goalkeeper last season, with eight clean sheets in 32 Premier League appearances (Confirmed, via Goal.com). During the October international break he started all three of Belgium’s matches, including a 3–0 win over Turkey on 2 October in which he kept a clean sheet (Confirmed, reported consistently across multiple Belgian outlets). Mark van Bommel, who previously managed Lammens at Royal Antwerp and is now Belgium’s head coach, started him ahead of Thibaut Courtois for the break (Confirmed, reported).

One further fact, confirmed as of this week: United have not kept a Premier League clean sheet all season — not against Hull, Ipswich, Everton, Manchester City or Fulham. That is a team and defensive record, not a save-by-save statistic on Lammens individually, and nobody has yet used it to make a specific case about him (Confirmed, via Read Man Utd, 4 October).

Who’s saying what

Paul Scholes doubts Lammens is title-winning quality: “What team have you seen win the Premier League without a top-class goalkeeper?” and “Lammens might be that, I don’t know, I think the jury’s probably still out on that.” He named Alisson, Raya, Schmeichel and van der Sar as the benchmark. The comment was originally made on a podcast in mid-September but was recirculated widely this week, tied to the City derby defeat (Analysis/punditry).

Roy Keane didn’t engage with Lammens’ goalkeeping at all. His criticism, made on The Overlap in what is now the “classic Roy Keane” style, was about conduct straight after the City loss: “I saw the goalkeeper after the match, and I know you’ve family and friends… he’s on the pitch getting his photograph with family and friends after the game. You’ve lost the derby game?” (Analysis/punditry, 28 September).

Lammens himself answered the ability question, not the conduct one: “I have not changed character by playing football at Manchester United,” and “You have to be mentally strong and self-confident in the face of all those people who express their opinions.” On his standing as Belgium’s current first-choice: “I think I am the number 1 now,” adding, “I cannot control what the future holds.” In a second press conference this week he added: “I can imagine that, for some players, it might change them a little bit.” (Attributed, interviews with Het Belang van Limburg and at a Belgium press conference, 2–4 October).

Mark van Bommel and the Belgian press were supportive on different grounds. Van Bommel praised Lammens’ temperament — he “always remains the same regardless of the circumstances” — and his quick adaptation to United. Three Belgian outlets rated his display against Turkey highly: Het Nieuwsblad (7/10, “near flawless”), Het Laatste Nieuws (7/10) and Voetbal Primeur (8/10, “another excellent match”). Worth noting: these are match ratings for his country, which speak to current form, not a verdict on his Premier League ceiling (Reported).

Where sources agree — and disagree

Nobody disputes the trigger: every source here, Lammens included, treats the derby defeat as the moment that reopened the goalkeeper question. Nobody claims he has been dropped or is under threat of being dropped by United — Carrick has made no public comment on this either way this week, so this is a punditry and player-response story, not a team-news one. And nobody disputes that Lammens played well for Belgium.

The real disagreement is over what the derby defeat actually says about him. Scholes treats it as part of a pattern that leaves his title-winning credentials in doubt. Keane doesn’t engage with the goalkeeping question at all — his complaint is about conduct in the aftermath, a different argument entirely, and Lammens hasn’t addressed it on the record anywhere this ledger could find. There’s a second, quieter disagreement underneath: van Bommel and the Belgian press treat his international form as evidence in his favour, but Scholes’ question was specifically about Premier League title-winning calibre — a different and higher bar — so the two bodies of evidence aren’t strictly answering the same question.

One further point worth flagging: United’s clean-sheet drought is a real, checkable fact that sits on Scholes’ side of the argument. But it covers five very different results with five different causes, and nobody quoted here has actually made that specific statistical case yet.

Part two: Can United keep JJ Gabriel?

JJ Gabriel turned 16 on 6 October. Within days, United had lodged a formal scholarship offer to try to keep him, The Guardian had reported he’s “almost certain” to leave anyway, Barcelona’s own sporting director had denied his club is even interested, and Michael Carrick had asked, in essence, that everyone remember he’s a child. Four different people, four different things to say — and for once, the facts underneath the story are unusually well sourced, not relayed at second hand.

What has been confirmed

Gabriel has been a United academy forward since age 11, having come through Chelsea’s, Arsenal’s and West Ham’s academies before that. Last month he formally asked to cancel his registration and leave United, citing a lack of first-team opportunities, and has not trained with the club for three weeks or more (Confirmed, multiple outlets).

United have now lodged a scholarship contract offer aimed at keeping him for next season. The Guardian’s Sam Cunningham, who broke the story, confirmed directly: “The Guardian can reveal that the offer has now been lodged with the player.” (Confirmed, The Guardian, 8 October). The offer matters legally as well as practically: without it, Gabriel could have left for nothing in January under Premier League youth rules. With it lodged, any Premier League club now has to negotiate a fee with United or have one set by the Professional Football Compensation Committee tribunal if talks fail. United would expect “in excess of £3m” plus a 20% sell-on clause from a tribunal if a Premier League club signs him; a move abroad would net United only around £350,000 under FIFA training-compensation rules (Confirmed, The Guardian). The precedent cited: Liverpool were ordered to pay Chelsea £2.8m upfront, plus up to £1.2m in performance add-ons and a 20% sell-on clause, for Rio Ngumoha (Confirmed, Read Man Utd).

United chief executive Omar Berrada and director of football Jason Wilcox are personally involved in talks with Gabriel’s family (Confirmed, SPORTbible). Gabriel is currently training privately with a coach in Castelldefels, Catalonia, while away from United’s facility (Confirmed, Manchester Evening News). Real Madrid are consistently reported as favourites to sign him; Bayern Munich and Paris Saint-Germain are monitoring the situation; he has already turned down Chelsea (Confirmed, reported consistently across every outlet opened).

This is the first season Gabriel is old enough to play in the Premier League. He played pre-season football for United’s first team under both Ruben Amorim and Michael Carrick but was left out of the Champions League squad and has made no senior competitive appearance; his representatives had reportedly earmarked the 25 October home game against Bournemouth as a potential first-team debut (Confirmed, Manchester Evening News).

One correction worth making plainly: United’s willingness to consider legal action is conditional, not automatic. The Guardian’s own wording is that the club “will consider taking legal action if they believe recruitment rules have been breached” — narrower than framing used elsewhere that United are “considering legal action if the teen star leaves.” The Guardian’s version, read directly, is the accurate one.

Who’s saying what

Sam Cunningham, The Guardian, broke the scholarship-offer story but frames United as likely to lose him anyway: the piece calls him a “prodigy” United risk losing for £350,000, and states he is “almost certain to leave” despite the offer (Reported, 8 October).

Deco, Barcelona’s sporting director, directly denies his own club’s reported interest: “There’s nothing to say about this boy… We are not interested in any player in his situation… to speak about young, teenage players, it is not our business.” (Attributed, via Manchester Evening News, 8 October).

Sky Sport Germany, via SPORTbible, reports Bayern Munich are in direct talks with Gabriel’s representatives over a package “well over” €10–15m — a transfer-package estimate, not the FIFA training-compensation figure United would actually receive. A single outlet’s report relayed via a second outlet, not independently verified (Reported, 8 October).

The Daily Mail, also via SPORTbible, frames Gabriel’s own motivation as developmental rather than a grievance: he wants to “learn from the best,” implying the pull is about squad quality and coaching rather than unhappiness with United specifically (Reported, attributed motivation, no direct quote from Gabriel).

Phil Bardsley, the former United and Scotland defender, speaking on talkSPORT, doubts Gabriel is ready for first-team football at all: “From the outside, physically I don’t think he’s ready to be thrown in at the level yet,” and blaming those around the player: “I believe it’s been orchestrated by agents, parents, and pushing for these things when you’re at such a delicate age.” (Analysis/punditry, via Manchester Evening News, 8 October).

Michael Carrick gave United’s only club-voice comment in this ledger: “I’ll just repeat what I said… he’s a young boy, it’s important we don’t put anything out here, the exposure and scrutiny we have to be careful. We have to be sensible and think about him. The situation will work itself out.” (Attributed, via Manchester Evening News, remarks made before the October international break).

Where sources agree — and disagree

Nobody disputes that Gabriel wants to leave, or that United have now made a formal offer to try to stop him. Every source also agrees a European move is dramatically cheaper for the buying club than a Premier League one. Real Madrid are reported as favourites everywhere this ledger looked, with Bayern and PSG described as monitoring rather than leading. And nobody disputes that Gabriel has yet to make a senior competitive appearance for United.

The disagreement splits three ways. On whether Barcelona are genuinely in the race: multiple reports list them among interested clubs, but Deco, their own sporting director, denies it directly and specifically — a named contradiction. On what’s driving Gabriel’s decision: the Guardian frames it as a dispute over playing time; the Daily Mail’s framing is softer — ambition rather than grievance; Bardsley offers a third explanation — orchestrated by agents and parents. Three different diagnoses. And on whether he’s ready for first-team football at all: the clubs chasing him implicitly treat him as ready; Bardsley explicitly disagrees.

THE UNITED ROUNDUP VIEW

Keane’s question is still unanswered. And Gabriel’s story is about the money.

On Lammens: Scholes and Keane aren’t actually arguing about the same thing, and lumping them together does the story no favours. Scholes’ question — is he title-winning class — hasn’t been answered either way yet; Lammens says yes, but saying it and proving it are two different jobs. Keane’s complaint is something else entirely: not about the goalkeeping, about reading the room after a bad result. And that’s the one nobody, Lammens included, has actually bothered to answer. What settles any of it: a clean sheet, or a performance that looks like one, against Tottenham on Saturday.

On Gabriel: strip away the noise and this one’s simple — it’s about the money, and that’s why United have moved now. Push a Premier League sale through a tribunal and they’re looking at £3m-plus. Let him walk to Europe and it’s £350,000. That gap is the whole story. What’s actually missing is Gabriel himself — nobody’s heard a word from him or his people on any of this, so every theory going round about what he wants is somebody else’s guess dressed up as insight. Carrick’s comment is worth taking seriously: he’s asking people to remember this is a 16-year-old with national newspapers putting transfer fees next to his name. That’s not an argument for going quiet on a story that’s already out there properly sourced — it is an argument for sticking to what’s actually confirmed and leaving the rest alone.

What comes next

Tottenham at Old Trafford on Saturday 10 October is the next test of the Lammens question. On Gabriel, no deadline for accepting or rejecting the scholarship terms has been reported; watch for any on-record statement from him or his representatives, and for what happens to the earmarked 25 October Bournemouth fixture if his situation hasn’t been resolved by then.
