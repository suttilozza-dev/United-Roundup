Apology from Martínez — but not for his words

Lisandro Martínez said United need more players and named the technical director. Then he said it again. Here's what every outlet is reporting, labelled.

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · Friday 17 October 2026

Lisandro Martínez, frustrated after a disappointing result in Madrid on Tuesday evening, unleashed a media frenzy around Manchester United with two post-match interviews and in the following days, two Instagram posts.

His post-match interviews, one with Amazon and one with CBS, fired shots at the club's lack of ambition calling out technical director Jason Wilcox by position. “We have to bring in more players to compete. I expect more from our technical director, I have to be honest.” And: “You can't keep changing, changing, changing the manager. What about other areas? Tonight it is easy to say the coach and easy to say the players, but also we have to see other areas.” He defended Carrick directly: “He does his best.” (Confirmed)

On Wednesday night, roughly 24 hours after the interview, Martínez posted a first statement on Instagram which was longer, more reflective, yet entirely consistent with what he'd said the night before. He said he believes “in my team-mates, our manager and everyone working to help this club move forward,” put himself “at the front” of responsibility for results, and framed his comments about wanting more as a demand for collective ambition rather than a personal attack. He did not apologise. He did not retract anything. (Confirmed.) Whatever this was, it was a position, stated twice. It wasn't his words that he regretted. Rather the way he decided to voice his grievances.

Twenty-four hours after that, following talks with club leadership and Carrick on Thursday, Martínez issued a third statement — this time with an apology. “The frustration of the defeat led me to express how I felt at a time and place that perhaps were not the right ones, and for that I want to apologise,” he said. He added that “my words in that moment did not help” and called the matter closed: “With this situation now behind us, we are fully focused on winning together.” He has been formally fined by the club. (Reported — BBC Sport, Sami Mokbel; Stretford Paddock, relaying Mokbel.) Carrick will hold a press conference on Friday ahead of the trip to Elland Road on Sunday. Roy Keane, on The Overlap podcast, described the situation at his former club as “a bit of a circus.” (Attributed — BBC Sport, 15 October.)

It seems like every forgotten corner of football media and punditry have emerged from the woodwork to throw their two cents in. The reactions to this week's big story came thick and fast in a short period of time and opinions are split roughly three ways: those who think the timing and method were wrong, those who think he didn't have the standing to say it at all, and those who think he was right and his critics are protecting the wrong people. What nobody is seriously disputing is the substance.

What has been confirmed

Atlético Madrid beat United 2-0 in the Champions League on 13 October. Pablo Barrios scored after 50 seconds, set up by Julián Álvarez and Alex Baena. Ademola Lookman added a second in the 79th minute. Leny Yoro was, questionably to some, sent off in the 74th for denying an obvious goal-scoring opportunity.

Sky Sports News was told there is “anger and bemusement” at the comments in both the boardroom and the changing room, and that at least one player confronted Martínez as the squad boarded the plane home. (Confirmed — reported, Sky Sports.) The Athletic, via Mario Cortegana and Laurie Whitwell, separately report that some players share his underlying frustration, though there was surprise at the manner and timing. Sir Jim Ratcliffe, Jason Wilcox and Omar Berrada were all at the game. Sources told The Athletic that none of the three will want the comments to go unchecked. (Confirmed — reported, The Athletic / New York Times.)

Martínez's contract expires next summer (June 2027); and United hold an option to extend it for a year. However, according to some reports, he does not want the option triggered, and United also have doubts about using it. His preference, if he leaves, would be Spain. Sources close to him say he would have made the same comments regardless of his contract situation — i.e. this was not an engineered exit attempt. (Confirmed — reported, The Athletic.) BBC Sport notes the club are still to decide whether to trigger the contract option, with significant doubt remaining over his long-term future at Old Trafford. (Confirmed — reported, BBC Sport.)

Who is saying what

The loudest immediate reaction came from the “timing” camp. Jamie Carragher called the comments “awful” and pointed straight back at Martínez's own recent form as the more pressing issue. (Attributed — CBS Sports.) Wayne Rooney agreed, calling them “disrespectful” to team-mates and arguing it should have stayed internal. (Attributed — Amazon Prime.) Jamie O'Hara went further, calling the comments “poor and shocking” and the act itself “stupid.” (Attributed — Sky Sports.) Carrick, for his part, sidestepped the substance entirely: “I haven't seen it... now is the time to be together.” (Attributed — Yahoo Sports.)

Ashley Young's objection sits in a slightly different place to Carragher and Rooney's. His issue wasn't the timing — it was the standing. “I don't feel like he's got the right,” Young said, a sharper claim that turns on Martínez's own form rather than the moment he chose. (Attributed — Sky Sports.) Rio Ferdinand was more circumspect, offering a warning rather than a verdict: “These things don't end well, man.” (Rio Ferdinand Presents.)

Two voices took a more considered middle position. Jurgen Klinsmann called the situation fixable but said Martínez was “double accountable” — for the performance and for the way he chose to communicate. (Attributed — ESPN FC.) James Westwood at Goal.com agreed the squad isn't deep enough and that Wilcox should be made to answer for it, but argued the outburst was self-serving and ought to result in a fine and a drop for Leeds. (Analysis-punditry — Goal.com.)

Jonathan Liew in The Guardian seemed to offer support for both Wilcox and Martínez. His argument is that the substance goes well beyond Wilcox: United couldn't buy a defender this summer because they couldn't sell one, and they couldn't sell because of a debt structure that precedes Wilcox by two decades. He called out Rooney, Young and Ferdinand by name as examples of focusing on the messenger rather than the message. (Analysis-punditry — The Guardian.) Vatsal Gupta at United In Focus read the Wednesday Instagram statement as a “diplomatic masterclass” — Martínez neither backed down nor apologised, just softened the language. Gupta noted, without endorsing it, that some fans see echoes of Ronaldo's 2022 exit interview: create noise, force the move, head for Spain. (Analysis-punditry — United In Focus.)

On Carrick's position, David Ornstein reported that ownership is backing the manager for now, but conditionally: “if you don't deliver, you'll pay the price.” (Attributed — The Athletic, via FourFourTwo.) Thursday's newspaper round-ups, as relayed by Sky Sports Paper Talk, added the usual paper-talk layer: The Sun named Brighton's Fabian Hurzeler as a candidate to replace Carrick should he be sacked, talkSPORT named Antonio Conte and Julian Nagelsmann as having admirers at Old Trafford. None of this is sourced reporting of an active process — it is contingency speculation, and it should be read as such. (Reported — Sky Sports Paper Talk.)

The Telegraph confirms United's declared three-phase rebuild: attack last year, midfield this summer, defence slated for next year. (Confirmed — reported, The Telegraph, James Ducker.)

On the back line heading into Leeds: Shaw, Dorgu and Mazraoui are all out. De Ligt was an unused substitute in Madrid in his first call up after nearly a year out. And Carrick played centre-back Ayden Heaven at left-back against Atlético. (Confirmed)

Where sources agree — and disagree

Agree. On the facts, nobody disagrees. The match result, Martínez's quotes, the statements and the summer transfer facts are all consistent across every outlet that has covered them. No source disputes that United signed three midfielders and no defenders this summer, or that United pursued defensive targets and couldn't complete them. No source reports that ownership has decided to act on Carrick.

The dressing-room picture also has more common ground than the noise suggests. Sky Sports and The Athletic are reporting from different sources and arrive at the same basic shape: some players share the underlying frustration, and some were genuinely angry about how Martínez chose to express it. Those two things can both be true.

Disagree — on framing. The real fault line is whether this is a Wilcox problem or a structural ownership problem. Martínez named Wilcox by position; most pundits kept the frame there. The Guardian and, more quietly, the Telegraph both push it outward — United couldn't buy because they couldn't sell, and they couldn't sell because of a debt structure that precedes Wilcox by two decades. That is a materially different argument about where responsibility sits, and it's double-sourced from credible journalists, not just one columnist's take.

Disagree — on standing. Carragher and Rooney object to the timing. Ashley Young's objection is different and sharper: he doesn't think Martínez had the right to say it at all given his own form. Liew explicitly argues that framing protects the people responsible for the problem rather than engaging with the problem itself.

Disagree — on motive. The Athletic's sources say the contract situation has nothing to do with it. Some fans, noted without endorsement by United In Focus, see the Ronaldo 2022 playbook: create noise, force the move, head for Spain. Neither reading is confirmed; both are live — and the contract situation remaining unresolved keeps both in play.

What remains unknown. Whether Martínez features against Leeds on Sunday — that is still to be confirmed. And whether any of this moves the needle on Wilcox's position; no source has reported any internal pressure on him. The fine is not confirmed by the club itself but highly likely and reported via multiple outlets; that question is still to be settled.

THE UNITED ROUNDUP VIEW

Opinion.

Martínez said something true in the wrong way, and now everyone is arguing about the way he came out, the ramifications and his motivations rather than the actual comments themselves. That's useful for the people he was criticising.

The substance isn't in dispute: We went into this season with one natural left-back, no striker cover, and the same back line from two or three seasons ago. Some of which will be out of contract next summer. The summer transfer window did add three midfielders, We signed three midfielders (Andrey Santos, Youri Tielemans, Carlos Baleba) and a reserve goalkeeper (Karl Darlow) however, nobody up front and nobody in defence. Many have already pointed out that the players that arrived weren't first choice acquisitions. United were supposed to be in for multiple top targets - Elliot Anderson, Mateus Fernandes, Aurélien Tchouaméni among some of the bigger names. Later in the window as options disappeared the shopping list was whittled down to Lewis Hall and Myles Lewis-Skelly but United still could not complete deals. It may have been a lack of ambition as Martínez claims or simply because they could not sell a player to fund them — there was no buying interest in Dalot, Mazraoui, Ugarte, Maguire, Zirkzee, Shaw, or Martínez himself.

Meanwhile Ayden Heaven, a centre-back, is playing left-back in the Champions League and we're still without a striker. The plan, according to the Telegraph, is for the defence to be addressed next summer.

Whether the failure sits with Wilcox, with Ratcliffe's budget ceiling, or with two decades of Glazer debt loading the club with interest costs rather than transfer funds — or all three, in layers — it's worth arguing about. But naming the wrong person in the right conversation is not the same as being wrong about the conversation — Martínez named Wilcox specifically by position, not by name, it has to be noted, and that's the most exposed claim, because neither Wilcox nor the club have responded to the substance of the Martínez post-match interviews as yet.

He said it after a defeat, in a post-match interview. It wasn't exactly a carefully prepared media strategy. So the timing argument is not really a valid argument in my view. What it was, per his own statement the following day, was how he feels — and the subsequent statement made clear he still feels it.

Whether Martínez plays against Leeds, whether the fine changes anything, and whether any of this moves the needle on Wilcox's position will tell us more than another day of pundits lining up to question his right to speak. Maybe it will all come out in the wash and this will prove to be the perfect catalyst for a more concerted effort for results by all at the club, players included. I don't want to see Carrick lose his job just yet, regardless of the results to date this season because we all know the reasons behind them do not solely rest with the manager. What the club needs is a sense of unity, stability and a work ethic that prime-Fergie would be proud of. I want the likes of Roy Keane, Garry Neville, Paul Scholes and alike to have no reason to criticise their former club.

I want to feel the togetherness through the screen as I watch every game, whatever the result. I want to hear Old Trafford rocking with anticipation at every touch — not booing as they leave their seats early.
