Manchester City Lodge Appeal as Premier League Reveals “Sham” Image-Rights Arrangement

City lodged their appeal on 1 October, within deadline. The Premier League’s own statement — not a secondhand summary — describes a “sham” circular arrangement run through an image-rights entity called Fordham. Richard Masters calls it the most significant disciplinary case in the competition’s history. Here is what is confirmed, what is reported, and when a resolution realistically arrives.

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · Thursday 9 October 2026

Follow-up: This piece covers developments since our 2 October Big Story on the verdict and appeal deadline. It should be read alongside that piece, not instead of it.

Manchester City have formally lodged their appeal against the 114-charge guilty verdict, and the Premier League’s own statement — not previously pulled directly for our coverage — adds a detail sharper than anything reported secondhand: the commission found a “sham” circular arrangement run through an image-rights entity called Fordham. Premier League chief executive Richard Masters has called it the most significant disciplinary case in the competition’s history. None of that resolves the case. It does sharpen exactly what City are appealing against, and roughly when the answer arrives.

The leading story

City lodged their appeal at 7pm on 1 October, on grounds of “clear material errors, of law, principle and fact” — confirmed by the Premier League (Confirmed). That meets the 2 October deadline reported in our previous piece, with hours to spare.

The Premier League’s own statement, now checked directly rather than via secondary reporting, describes the commission’s finding of a “sham” circular arrangement routed through Fordham, an image-rights entity, as part of the wider “Tagged Sums” picture already reported (Confirmed). Richard Masters called the case the most significant disciplinary matter in the Premier League’s history (Confirmed).

On timing: the Premier League’s new fast-track rules require an appeal hearing within 12 weeks of lodging, putting a ruling by the end of January 2027 at the latest, assuming those rules are upheld (Reported — Flashscore). City are expected to argue the fast-track rules were not yet in force when they were originally charged in February 2023, which could push the hearing to a later date (Reported — Flashscore). Rival clubs are reportedly pushing for a decision before the end of the season and want the sentencing process to run alongside the appeal rather than after it, to avoid further delay (Reported — SportsMole); Ferran Soriano has warned the process could take considerably longer than the fast-track timeline suggests (Attributed — Soriano, via SportsMole).

The other key developments

Rival clubs are reportedly planning for City’s absence. Sky Sports reports that rival clubs — unnamed — do not expect City to be in the Premier League next season if the appeal fails, and believe a points deduction alone would not be a sufficient sanction (Reported — Sky Sports, sourcing anonymous). That is other clubs’ expectation, not a Premier League or City position, and it sits well ahead of any sanctions hearing even being scheduled.

What hasn’t moved. Two threads from our 2 October piece remain exactly where they were: no date has been set for the separate sanctions hearing, and Etihad Airways’ “considering legal action” position has not visibly progressed since its late-September statement (Confirmed, by absence of any update found).

Where sources agree — and disagree

Agree. There is no conflict on the sourced facts: the appeal was lodged on 1 October, within deadline; the Premier League’s own statement confirms the Fordham arrangement and Masters’ “most significant” characterisation; the 12-week fast-track rule and the end-of-January 2027 outer limit are reported consistently across outlets; and nobody disputes that the sanctions hearing remains unscheduled.

Disagree — or rather, unresolved. There is a live tension in the reporting about how fast this actually moves. Flashscore’s framing puts the ruling “before the end of January 2027 at the latest”, assuming the fast-track rules hold — but notes City are expected to argue those rules did not apply when they were charged in 2023, which could delay the hearing. SportsMole’s timeline is tighter at the front end, with a theoretical 25 December deadline for the hearing to conclude if City appealed on 2 October, and a final decision arriving in January — but it also reports disagreement over whether sentencing should run alongside the appeal or wait for it, and relays Soriano’s own warning that the process could take considerably longer. None of this is a factual contradiction; it is uncertainty about process that the sources themselves flag as unresolved.

What would settle it. Confirmation of whether City are contesting the fast-track rules’ applicability; any Premier League statement on whether sentencing will run alongside the appeal; and a sanctions hearing date, whenever one is set.

What it means

The headline fact for United supporters is the same as on 2 October: this changes the competitive landscape without changing United’s own position in it. What is new is that the case now has a plausible resolution window — realistically Christmas to late January 2027, with a real chance of slipping further if City contest the fast-track rules — meaning the uncertainty is likely to run through most of the season rather than resolve quickly.

THE UNITED ROUNDUP VIEW

The appeal was expected. The PL’s own language was not.

The appeal being lodged on time, and on the grounds the club already previewed, is not itself news about guilt or innocence — it is process, and it was always going to happen. The more significant item this week is evidential: the Premier League’s own statement, not a secondhand summary, now describes a “sham” arrangement in its own words, and that is a stronger claim than “overstated sponsorship income” on its own. The timeline reported here suggests supporters should expect this to run close to, or past, Christmas regardless of outcome, and probably should not expect a tidy early resolution. Whether City’s challenge to the fast-track rules succeeds is itself now a thing to watch — it would say something about how seriously the process is being contested on procedure rather than substance.

What comes next

Watch for: confirmation of a sanctions hearing date; any Premier League or City statement on the fast-track rules dispute; and any movement from Etihad. United Roundup will update this piece as confirmed information becomes available.
