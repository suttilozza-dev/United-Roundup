Manchester City Found Guilty on 114 of 115 Charges: What Happens Next

An independent commission has found City guilty on 114 of 115 financial charges. The appeal deadline is today, no sanctions hearing has been scheduled, and the penalty is still unknown.

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · Friday 2 October 2026

An independent commission has found Manchester City guilty on 114 of the 115 Premier League financial charges brought against them, in a verdict delivered on 29–30 September. One charge was rejected, three cooperation breaches were upheld, and City now have until today — 2 October — to lodge an appeal. For a competition City have dominated for a decade, the ruling reopens a question that was never fully settled by the 2023 Champions League win: whether the financial foundations of that success were built honestly.

The leading story

The commission’s 40-page Core Decision, reported via the psrwatch.com summary, found that City’s income had been “hugely overstated by over £830m” through what it calls “Tagged Sums” — payments from Abu Dhabi United Group recorded in the accounts as commercial sponsorship. One example given: in 2017/18, City recorded £145.73m in sponsorship income against actual sponsor payments of £11m (Confirmed).

City will appeal. The club’s statement called itself “disappointed and surprised,” insisted it holds “a comprehensive body of irrefutable evidence” to support its position, and said the appeal would cite “material errors, of law, principle and fact” (Confirmed). The deadline for lodging that appeal is today, 2 October, heard by a fresh three-person commission under Section W of the Premier League rules. There is no route to the Court of Arbitration for Sport; a High Court challenge remains theoretically possible, but only on procedural grounds (Confirmed, via Sky Sports). No sanctions hearing has yet been scheduled, and no fixed penalty grid exists for a case of this scale (Confirmed, via Sky Sports).

The other key developments

Etihad distances itself from City’s case. Etihad Airways, the club’s shirt and stadium sponsor and the airline whose payments sit at the centre of the “Tagged Sums” finding, said it would “seek relevant legal counsel… to protect its interests,” rejected any suggestion of improper commercial arrangements, and criticised the Premier League for not contacting it before publication (Confirmed, Etihad Airways statement). That is a legal threat aimed at the Premier League, not at City — but it signals the sponsor is not simply standing behind the club’s account of events.

Leadership reportedly knew before the summer spend. ESPN, via the psrwatch.com summary, reported that City’s leadership learned of the guilty findings internally in July — before the club committed more than £450m in summer transfer spending (Reported). United Roundup has found no direct City rebuttal of this report.

Reaction from within the club. Pep Guardiola, who has since departed as City manager, posted a message of support: “I am here; more than ever… we will get through this together” (Confirmed). Chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak’s open letter to supporters said “our confidence and intent in proving the Club’s innocence is just as strong” (Reported, via psrwatch.com). CEO Ferran Soriano was reported by Sky Sports to have called the process a “Premier League conspiracy theory” in a leaked internal video (Reported).

Where sources agree — and disagree

The core facts are not in dispute. Every outlet reviewed, and City’s own statement, agree on the 114/115 verdict, the single rejected charge, the three upheld cooperation breaches, the £830m “Tagged Sums” finding, and the 2 October appeal deadline.

The disagreement is over framing, not facts. City and Soriano describe the process as unfair, even conspiratorial. Against that, former City chairman David Bernstein called the findings “damning beyond belief” and said “if the appeal fails, Manchester City cannot stay in the league” (Attributed). Former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness predicted a points deduction “up to 50,” which “would probably ensure relegation” (Attributed) — a specific figure that remains Speculation, since no sanctions hearing has taken place and no penalty has been set. Former City financial adviser Stefan Borson described the club’s early non-cooperation as “a major aggravating factor” for sentencing (Attributed). LaLiga president Javier Tebas called for a “severe” punishment (Attributed), and Jamie Carragher argued City’s titles should be stripped and ownership removed — his personal opinion, not a reported fact (Attributed).

What remains genuinely unknown: whether City lodged the appeal today, as no confirmation or denial had surfaced at the time of writing; whether a sanctions hearing date has since been set; and what penalty, if any, follows. Any figure for a points deduction, and any suggestion that relegation, a transfer ban or a fine is the likely outcome, is Speculation until a sanctions hearing happens.

What it means

For Manchester United, this is background rather than a direct storyline — but it is far from irrelevant background. A rival with City’s financial scale facing a verdict of this severity changes the competitive picture regardless of the eventual sanction, and the appeal timeline (reported running to around late January 2027) means the uncertainty sits over a large part of this season. United Roundup is not treating this as a City story to needle a rival with; it is a Premier League governance story that affects every club in the division, United included, and we will keep tracking the process on that basis.

THE UNITED ROUNDUP VIEW

The verdict is fact. The sanction is not.

The verdict itself is not in question: City were found guilty on 114 of 115 charges, by an independent commission, and the club’s own statement confirms as much even while disputing the conclusion. What cannot yet be claimed as fact is the outcome — any points deduction, relegation scenario or transfer ban is speculation dressed as inevitability by some commentators, and United Roundup won’t repeat specific sanction figures as if they’re settled. The fair position is this: the financial findings are serious and largely uncontested, the sanction is genuinely unknown, and the appeal process — not punditry — will decide what happens next.

What comes next

City’s appeal deadline falls today, 2 October. No sanctions hearing has been scheduled. The appeal process is reported to run to around late January 2027. United Roundup will confirm whether the appeal was lodged and report any sanctions hearing date once one is set.
