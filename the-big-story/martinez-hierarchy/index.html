‘What about other areas?’: Martínez calls out the hierarchy, and United have yet to answer

Martínez named United’s technical director on Amazon Prime after a 2-0 defeat in Madrid. The quotes are agreed. Whether he was right to say it is not.

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · Wednesday 14 October 2026

Manchester United lost 2-0 to Atlético Madrid in the Champions League on Tuesday night, Pablo Barrios scoring inside 53 seconds and Ademola Lookman adding a second after Leny Yoro was sent off. It was an all too familiar sounding story: an early defensive error, chances created and wasted, questions about the back line, and then the press conference. Except then Lisandro Martínez spoke.

The Argentina defender used his post-match interview on Amazon Prime to say publicly what the evidence, fans, pundits and journalists alike have pointed to since July: that United entered the season without a left-back or a striker, that the decision sat with people above the coaching staff, and that repeatedly changing managers while leaving “other areas” untouched is not the answer. Martinez named Jason Wilcox by position but stopped himself twice before saying more. BBC Sport’s Simon Stone posted one line to X as the interview circulated:

(Attributed — Simon Stone, @sistoney67, 14 October 2026.) Nothing from the club at the time of writing.

United have extended their winless run to five games and have won twice from nine in all competitions. (Reported — Talk of the Devils/The Athletic, 13 October; Sky Sports, 14 October.)

The leading story

Quotes come from primary source — the Amazon Prime broadcast — and have since been confirmed by the Guardian, Sky Sports and CBS Sports.

Martínez described the result as “a reflection of the mirror of how is our club at the moment”, said he expected more “from the top, from up, to bring more players to compete”, confirmed “100%” that he expected more investment in the squad, named “our technical director” specifically, acknowledged that “as players we have to do more” but said a strong squad is also required, defended Michael Carrick (“he does his best”), and closed with: “You start changing, changing, changing manager, and but what about other areas? I can’t say more.” He finished: “Tonight it is easy to say the coach, easy to say the players, but also we have to see other areas.” (Confirmed — Amazon Prime post-match broadcast, 13 October 2026.)

Youri Tielemans, one of the three outfield summer signings, was asked by Amazon to respond immediately afterwards. He said nothing — described by Sky Sports as a flat refusal to comment. (Confirmed — Sky Sports, 14 October.)

Michael Carrick, in separate post-match interviews with Amazon and the Guardian, said he had not seen the interview and was not prepared to comment on it. To Amazon: “Straight after a game for me is not the time to talk about that interview because I haven’t seen it, so I can’t really comment on that.” To the Guardian: “I think it’s a little bit the emotions of the game... I have to find out a bit more about it to be honest to decide what and if anything is going to be done.” (Attributed — Carrick, Amazon Prime; Guardian, 13 October.) He added that the group needed to “move forward quickly” ahead of Sunday. In a separate post-match comment relayed by Stretford Paddock, he described the mood inside the squad: “The boys are hurt. They are passionate; they want to do well.” In a second clip from the same source, Carrick addressed Martínez’s description of him — “he does his best” — as a form of backing: “I don’t doubt the togetherness.” (Attributed — Carrick, via Stretford Paddock, 13 October.)

Jason Wilcox, Sir Jim Ratcliffe and the club had not publicly responded at time of writing.

The game

Before the interview consumed everything, there was a match. Barrios scored after 53 seconds. (Confirmed — Guardian.) United, having conceded that early in front of a partisan crowd, never fully settled — but they did create. Carrick told the Guardian there were “four almost one-vs-ones” that United did not convert. Aiden Heaven started at left-back on his Champions League debut; Bruno Fernandes operated on the right and hit the crossbar; Tielemans played in a number-10 role; Baleba and Andrey Santos were described by both the Talk of the Devils panel and the CBS pundits as among the better performers on the night. (Reported — Talk of the Devils/The Athletic; CBS Sports broadcast, 13 October.)

Yoro’s sending-off — for a challenge on Jonathan David — was described by Carrick as “a poor decision.” The Talk of the Devils panel called it “very harsh.” The CBS pundits were split: Michael Owen said it was “very soft”, with a covering defender also present; another panellist noted that last man is last man and contact was made. (Reported — Talk of the Devils/The Athletic; mixed analysis — CBS broadcast.) Lookman added the second after United were reduced to ten.

Where sources agree — and disagree

Agree. Every outlet confirms the same Martínez quotes from Amazon Prime. And also agree that the comments reflect the general sentiment around the club from supporters, reporting and punditry analysis. Nobody disputes that Wilcox was the named target. Simon Stone’s X post reflects a consensus across the coverage that the direction of the criticism is unambiguous. All outlets confirm Carrick had not seen the interview and declined to comment substantively. All confirm Tielemans refused to engage. The scorers, the red card, and the basic shape of the game are reported consistently. Every source closed on the same unanswered thread: what the club does next, who says it — Carrick, Wilcox, or someone further up — and what it means for a manager who has just lost again.

Disagree on whether Martínez was right to say it. Wayne Rooney, on Amazon as a pundit, said the comments went “a bit too far” and were “a bit disrespectful to his team-mates”, while conceding “in some ways he is right in terms of the club needs to invest more.” Jamie Carragher, on CBS Sports, called it “awful that he’s come out and said that right now”, disputed the “fantastic season” framing — a manager was sacked last season; United finished third partly because Liverpool and Chelsea had an unusually poor year; Champions League absences had constrained the budget — and said “that interview is not going to help the situation.” Sky Sports’ Zinny Boswell took the opposite editorial line: “It is hard to argue with his point.” Andy Mitten, on Talk of the Devils/The Athletic, called it “a grenade” and said Martínez had “caught the mood of a lot of fans.”

Disagree on the performance. Carrick said the game was “there for us” and that “the players have been great.” The Talk of the Devils panel described it as “probably one of United’s better performances this season, particularly given the circumstances, given the stadium, given the opposition.” The CBS broadcast was more critical, describing United as “all over the place defensively” and questioning Carrick’s substitution timing around the hour mark. Fan channel analysis reviewed was harder still: with two wins from nine, individual performance positives look different when you zoom out.

Disagree on the red card. A question of interpretation, not fact. Carrick: poor decision. Talk of the Devils: harsh. CBS pundits: split, with Owen leaning lenient and others defending the decision.

What would settle it. A response from the club, Wilcox or Ratcliffe. Confirmation of whether Martínez is in the squad for Leeds on Sunday. Carrick’s pre-Leeds press conference — expected Thursday or Friday — is where most of this must land.

What it means

Martínez named a person. That is the part that distinguishes this from general discontent. Laurie Whitwell of The Athletic, who was in Madrid, described it on Talk of the Devils as “unprecedented really.” Players have muttered about recruitment before. A manager — Amorim — raised structural concerns in press conferences and was gone not long after. For a player to name the technical director specifically, on live television, in the immediate aftermath of a European away defeat, is new territory.

It is also worth noting the gap between the two post-match accounts. Carrick’s “the boys are hurt, they are passionate, they want to do well” and Martínez’s Amazon Prime interview were both responses to the same defeat, on the same night. They are not describing the same dressing room.

The club now has no clean option. Act against Martínez and a fanbase that broadly agrees with him, and a dressing room whose summer mood was reported to be similar, may read it as confirmation. Say nothing and the comments stand unanswered. Wilcox responds publicly and becomes the story himself. Silence buys time; it does not answer the question.

There is also the matter of Martínez’s contract, which runs out at the end of the season. United hold an option for a further year. (Reported — Talk of the Devils/The Athletic.) Whether they exercise it has just become one of the more loaded decisions in the building.

THE UNITED ROUNDUP VIEW

Opinion.

The interview was extraordinary. The content was not. What Martínez said on Amazon Prime is what the evidence has suggested since well before August and what several outlets — this one included — have noted: United went into this season structurally short in the two positions that matter most, and that was a choice made by people above the coaching staff. Carragher is right that the “fantastic season” framing overstates what the third place finish represented. Rooney is right that there is a better time and place for this conversation. Neither of them is saying the underlying complaint is wrong, and that matters.

The complication is the messenger. Martínez is in the final year of his contract with his extension option unresolved. He was substituted in the first half of the very game that prompted the interview, and his own positioning contributed to the opening goal. The things players say when their future is uncertain and the camera is running are worth hearing and worth weighing at the same time. The point stands. The timing and the situation colour it.

I’m somehow reminded of the time Ralf Rangnick came out in a press conference with the now infamous “Open-Heart Surgery” analogy. I think to myself after these Martinez comments, that this analogy makes more sense the more time passes. Surgery sometimes leaves scar tissue, initially part of the healing, but then ultimately has to be removed as it no longer serves the body as a whole. We have to wonder whether it is scar tissue removal time at United and certain positions within the hierarchy are not serving the club as a whole. The same questions have now been asked for a very extended period of time and the solutions from the very “scar-tissue” positions have generally been to sack the manager. How many managers must pass through the doors before a different approach is considered?

What United say next is the only thing that changes any of this. Not to Martínez — to their supporters, who have watched the same structural problem play out across multiple managers and multiple windows. A response better than silence is owed. Not because a player asked for one loudly. But because the evidence already has.

What comes next

Leeds away on Sunday 18 October, kick-off 2pm UK time (Confirmed — Sky Sports). Carrick’s pre-Leeds press conference is expected on Thursday or Friday. Watch for any club statement on Martínez before that; any indication of whether he is in the squad for Leeds; and whether Wilcox, Ratcliffe or anyone from the board speaks publicly. The Champions League continues with Como away on 22 October (Reported — MEN, as covered in WR05).
