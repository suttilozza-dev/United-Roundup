About United Roundup

Built for supporters who want the full picture.

United Roundup is an independent Manchester United news and analysis hub, founded and edited by Laurie Sutcliffe. We gather the day's reporting in one place, show exactly who is saying what and add the clear human explanation that helps supporters make sense of it.

Our editorial principle

“Automate discovery. Never judgement.”

Software can find and organise reporting quickly. Deciding what is credible, important and fair remains an editorial responsibility.

Why we exist

Following United should not mean opening twenty tabs.

Manchester United is covered by the club itself, local and national newsrooms, specialist reporters, transfer journalists, former players, fan publications and a huge video community. That breadth is valuable—but it can also make a fast-moving story difficult to follow. United Roundup is designed to be the useful middle ground: broad enough to show the whole conversation, careful enough to distinguish fact from noise, and concise enough to become part of a supporter's daily routine.

How the desk works

Technology does the searching. A person does the publishing.

01Discover

Our tools monitor public club channels, established newsrooms, dedicated journalists, interviews and video publishers for relevant Manchester United coverage.

02Check

The original report is reviewed for its source, wording, context and whether a claim is confirmed, attributed, opinion or speculation.

03Compare

When several people are reporting the same story, we bring the different accounts together so readers can see where they agree—and where they do not.

04Explain

United Roundup adds a concise human view of what matters, what remains uncertain and what supporters should watch next.

What readers can expect

Useful coverage, with the labels left on.

We do not pretend every claim carries equal weight. The aim is to make the source, status and context of a story easy to understand.

Daily briefingOur editorial contribution

A concise original briefing that connects the day's biggest themes, competing reports and important context.

Open attributionThe reporter stays visible

Journalists, outlets and creators are clearly credited, with readers directed to the original publication for the complete work.

The whole clubMore than transfer headlines

First-team news sits alongside injuries, contracts, interviews, form, women's football, youth teams and Academy development.

AccountabilityCorrections are welcomed

If we get something materially wrong, we want to know. Corrections can be sent directly to our dedicated address.
