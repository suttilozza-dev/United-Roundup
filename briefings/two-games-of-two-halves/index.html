Two Games of Two Halves

Two similar performances - Two very different results.

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · Saturday 7 November 2026 (covering 31 October–7 November)

Right, where do we start? Some weeks you need a scorecard to keep up, and this was one of them.

United lost 2-0 at Chelsea last weekend with Lisandro Martínez sent off, putting what looked to many to be the final nail in the coffin for Carrick’s tenure as head coach. Then on Tuesday night United beat Roma 3-2 from two goals down, with Matheus Cunha scoring twice then getting himself sent off, and 19-year-old academy break-through Shea Lacey setting up the winner. Other club news includes a 16-year-old academy lad looking to be heading for Real Madrid, Avram Glazer told a banking conference that United’s commercial power doesn’t rest on winning matches, and Bruno Fernandes said on Amazon Prime that something the media reported never happened.

Aston Villa visit Old Trafford on Sunday, United are seventeenth, and the question every outlet has circled since Tuesday is whether the Roma comeback bought Michael Carrick time or just moved the verdict back a few days.

Everything below was written before kick-off.

At Chelsea, United were level at the break and, by The Telegraph’s account, shading it [44]. Against Roma they were two down and booed off, and the Guardian’s blog called the opening 45 minutes mediocre [2]. In the first half against Roma, United were outclassed and outrun. Carrick's tactics seemed to be all wrong and wasn’t helped by some terrible defending at the back. Again United went in at the break to the sound of boos.

First Half

United missed a few chances to go ahead, notably Bruno Fernandes putting the ball over the bar following a surging counter attack and a nice diagonal through ball along the ground from Brian Mbeumo. Throughout the first half United played well on the counter but a lack of confident thinking on the ball saw the Reds go in at the break level.

On Tuesday however, Roma were ahead through Soulé by the eleventh minute when Diogo Dalot failed to track back, the reason for which is being pointed to instructions from the manager, and when Aiden Heaven didn't spot the danger and was dragged inside to cover the man Harry Maguire should really have picked up, It left Soulé with acres of space and time to pick his shot. Then Roma doubled their lead after Aiden Heaven gave away a penalty from a handball in the box on the sixteenth, which Malen confidently slotted past Senne Lammens to make it 2-0 to the visitors, a lead they held to half time.

Second Half

After the restart it flipped. Chelsea took the lead just after half-time when Morgan Rogers went on a run with the ball, wrong-footed defenders and chipped the keeper [9][10][11]. Martínez, booked for a foul on Rogers, thrust an arm into Wesley Fofana’s face at a corner about a minute and a half later, was shown a second yellow, and Cole Palmer scored the penalty [9][10][11][44]. The BBC’s Simon Stone built his match piece around those 83 seconds [9]. It made nine games without a win in all competitions [9][10][11][12], and the BBC has United above the relegation zone on goal difference alone [9][10]. The table agrees: United are 17th on seven points, level with 18th-placed Crystal Palace and ahead of them only on goal difference, with bottom club Sunderland two points behind on five [52].

At Old Trafford, Carrick had some motivating to do with the players during the break and made clear changes to position and then later, personnel. United pulled one back in the 58th minute, levelled in the 83rd and won it in the 86th [2]. Against Roma the reports record a retaken penalty after Roma encroached, a Balerdi mistake for the equaliser and a Lacey pass for the winner [2][1]. The Guardian’s blog said United were fortunate with the retake and the equaliser, but earned them with hard work [2].

What swung each game was different. At Chelsea, in The Telegraph’s account, it came from United’s own defending: Rogers went between Maguire and Martínez, and Martínez was then sent off [44]. The pundits split in tone. Danny Murphy called Martínez “a liability” and Joe Cole said his time at United “has run its course” [9]. Pat Nevin’s BBC column was kinder, arguing that Martínez’s style of defending is increasingly punished by modern refereeing and VAR and that there is “probably nothing Martinez can do about his predicament” [13].

The crowds swung too. Chelsea’s supporters went from taunting Carrick to singing that they wanted him to stay [44]; Old Trafford was chanting his name by the 89th minute, with the anti-Glazer chants back in stoppage time [2]. The sources differ in three small places and we have left each as reported. TNT, quoted in The Telegraph’s blog, has 82 seconds between Martínez’s yellow cards, while the BBC’s headline says 83 [44][9]. The tennis balls are logged at 22 minutes on the Guardian’s live blog, at 20 minutes and five seconds in another Guardian report, in the 20th minute by The Athletic and the 21st by The National [2][58][59][60]. And The Telegraph has United 2-0 down after 17 minutes where the Guardian’s blog times Malen’s penalty in the 18th [53][2].

Injuries: Baleba out for weeks

Carlos Baleba, making his first start next to Kobbie Mainoo, went off with a hamstring injury against Roma. The MEN has the pairing lasting one half [25]; The Athletic has him forced off in the 51st minute [51]. Carrick said it “doesn’t look great” [25][26]. At his pre-Villa press conference on Friday he said “we don’t know exactly, but it’ll be a number of weeks”, adding that it “won’t be any time over the international break” [28][51]. The Sun reports he is out “until next month” [27]. Patrick Dorgu has “done a little bit of training this week”, Carrick said, though only lightly, and his Villa involvement is undecided [28][51]. Carrick gave no update on Shaw or Rashford, who are managing injuries [51]; the Guardian earlier said Rashford’s hip injury had no firm return date [29].

The National’s Andy Mitten reports that Danny Welbeck (it writes “Daniel”) was of interest in the summer but wanted two years, while United would not go past one year plus a £5m fee. It names no source. Mitten adds that a striker, probably on loan, is likely in January [60].

Ferguson at 40

Just a short piece here as we are planning an article for this great man, a paragraph doesn’t do him justice.

Friday 6 November was 40 years since United appointed Sir Alex Ferguson to replace Ron Atkinson [30][31]. A reporter raised the Mark Robins comparison, the 1990 FA Cup goal at Nottingham Forest said to have saved Ferguson’s job, and asked how much the Roma win could be a moment for the team. Carrick replied: “More comparisons. You always find something to compare!” [28] He added: “It’s up to us to take it forward” [28][51]. The Sun drew the same parallel in its own piece [27].

  • Howe and Nagelsmann are hedged. The Guardian says contact with Howe is “understood to have been made”, that he is open to the job subject to negotiations and would cost about £3m in compensation, and that Vivell is believed to have sounded out Nagelsmann [33].
  • The widest claim is the Mail’s, and it names no source: United’s bosses have been “privately talking to possible successors for weeks” [35]. Ronay wrote in the Guardian on 31 October that alternatives are being lined up and terms thrashed out, with no sourcing [42], while Whitwell says he is not aware of anything advanced [41]. “Talking to” is not the same as approached, and nobody says who spoke to whom.
  • The rest is interest, not contact. The Mail says Conte has indicated before that he would like to restore a fallen giant, reports scepticism that Hürzeler is gettable, and has Emery meeting Villa chiefs on Wednesday about a new deal [35]. Carragher, who floated Emery, accepts he is close to signing at Villa [57], and a United In Focus column argues Ineos should chase Deschamps anyway [63].
  • Whether Gabriel’s move is confirmed. Marca says Real are still finalising terms and that an announcement may wait until January [61].
  • How long Baleba is out: Carrick says “a number of weeks”, The Sun says “until next month” [28][27].
  • What the club says about the Fernandes talks.

The United Roundup view

Opinion

Most pundits were talking as if the end was all but certain and Michael Carrick’s fate was sealed when the final whistle blew at Stanford bridge. Another defeat, no goals and performance in the second half that told us that United couldn’t put a performance in for 90 minutes, couldn’t come back from behind in a game and the majority of them weren't up to the job. In contrast to the Chelsea result, Tuesday was a good night. It wasn’t a fix. United were two down inside 18 minutes and booed off, and they got back into it because the shape changed at half-time and Shea Lacey impressed in his fifteen minutes after coming on and Matheus Cunha got better as the half unfolded.

Overall that’s decent management but an awful way to start a game. I’d give Michael Carrick credit for the prior without pretending the latter isn’t still an issue.

What would change my mind is Villa on Sunday. If United look like the second half against Roma, Carrick has earned more time. If they look like the first, the question stops being whether he survives the break and becomes why the same problems keep coming back.

I’d be careful with Gabriel and Lacey. It’s two lads. One, a 16-year-old that looks to be leaving Manchester for Madrid and the other, a 19-year-old that has just broken into the United first team. There is a story in there about the academy’s player pathway criticism, but if United want to prove anything, Lacey starting on merit says more than any statement the club puts out.

On Bruno and the Guardian, I’m keeping my powder dry. A named paper says one thing, the player says the opposite, and none of us can see the conversation. Until somebody in the building says what happened, I’m not calling anyone a liar, and I’d ask anyone who is - what they know that we don’t.

As for Avram Glazer, whatever the business case, the timing of those comments reaching the public couldn’t have been worse. To me it smacks of a disconnected and disrespectful ownership. One that has zero emotional ties to the club or its supporters. Money is definitely the route of this evil.

What comes next

Aston Villa, whos own manager has been touted as a possible replacement for Carrick, visit Old Trafford on Sunday with Martínez suspended. Villa lost 3-0 at Barcelona on the night United beat Roma, and Emery said afterwards his side needed to be “more ruthless” [4]. The MEN’s five players Carrick “must start” are Lammens, Yoro, Mainoo, Lacey and Cunha [19]. I’d be inclined to agree with them on that. talkSPORT, via Sky, says Lacey is set for a full league debut [36], and The National says further protests are planned for Sunday [60].

After the international break, Liverpool away and Sporting Lisbon away [27]. Watch for any word from the club on Carrick, a Dorgu update, a timescale for Baleba, and anything from either club on Gabriel.
