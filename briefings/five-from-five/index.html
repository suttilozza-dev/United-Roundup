WEEKLY ROUNDUP

Five points from five, and a manager insisting the panic is bigger than the problem

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · 22 September 2026 · covering the week to Monday 21 September

Manchester United go into the international break twelfth in the Premier League with five points from five games, a tally that equals the club’s worst start to a Premier League season, set in 2014–15. Sky Sports’ match report and Al Jazeera both put United ten points behind leaders Manchester City already. It is September, and the gap is a double-figure one.

The week did the damage in two instalments. On Wednesday, United led Brighton 2-0 at Old Trafford in the Carabao Cup and lost 3-2 — the first time in roughly fifty years they have lost a home game after going two goals up. On Sunday at Craven Cottage they went behind to a Lisandro Martínez own goal and needed Matheus Cunha in the 89th minute to rescue a point against a Fulham side still without a league win this season.

What makes this week worth writing about is not the arithmetic but the argument that has broken out around it. Michael Carrick says the margins are fine and the reaction is overblown. Gary Neville, watching the same ninety minutes, called United’s second-half display “a genuine disgrace.” Both men are describing the same football match. Supporters are entitled to want to know which reading the evidence supports.

The leading story

The Fulham draw is the confirmed centre of it. Calvin Bassey’s shot was parried by Senne Lammens and turned into his own net by Martínez on 63 minutes; Cunha’s deflected strike on 89 salvaged the point (Sky Sports). United hit the woodwork twice — Bruno Fernandes in the first half, Cunha later — and Bernd Leno made several saves. Carrick’s post-match position was that United were the better side: “I thought we should’ve won the game, we created enough to win the game.”

Neville’s reading, given on Sky, was the opposite. He said it was “the worst 15 minutes I have ever seen Manchester United play,” that he had “never seen a Man Utd team walk out of possession as much as that,” and that Carrick “has got to stop defending them.” He also made a point that sits slightly apart from the outrage and is probably the more useful one: “You’ve got these five or six players who are better with the ball than without the ball… there is going to have to be a reset from the point of view of defensive structure.”

Notably, the dissent was not confined to the studio. Cunha, who scored the equaliser, told reporters that Fulham “put in more energy than us” and that the standard was not acceptable. When a scorer says that, the “outside noise” framing gets harder to sustain.

Three days earlier, the Brighton defeat had already set the tone. Shea Lacey, a 19-year-old making his first start, scored inside eight minutes and Mason Mount added a second shortly after; Charalampos Kostoulas pulled one back before half-time, Pascal Gross equalised on 65 and Maxim De Cuyper completed it on 70 (TNT Sports). Carrick’s own verdict: “We had it exactly where we wanted it and let it get away, in a big way.” Out of the cup at the first attempt, and a fifty-year record broken.

The other key developments

The injury list thinned an already thin attack. Benjamin Šeško has withdrawn from the Slovenia squad with a recurrence of the shin problem that troubled him at the end of last season, with United confirming he “will undergo tests this week before defining his individual recovery plan” (beIN Sports). He has started twice all season. Carrick said before Fulham that he was “not sure with Ben yet.” Marcus Rashford, who limped off on 77 minutes at Fulham, and Kobbie Mainoo were both named among five England withdrawals on 21 September, the FA confirming that “Tino Livramento, Kobbie Mainoo, Cole Palmer, Marcus Rashford and Declan Rice have all been ruled out” of the Nations League fixtures. Matthijs de Ligt travelled to London but was not fit to be selected; Carlos Baleba made the bench for the first time after an ankle problem; Luke Shaw returned to the starting XI; Amad Diallo is still without an appearance this season.

Carrick’s position was briefed on, not decided. Multiple outlets reported during the week that United’s hierarchy has no intention of making a change during the break. That reporting is covered in the section below, because the sourcing matters more than the headline.

Confirmed club news was thin, as it usually is in a break week. Two November fixtures have moved for television: Aston Villa at Old Trafford is now Sunday 8 November, 16:30, and Liverpool away is Sunday 22 November, 16:30, both confirmed by the club and by the Premier League’s November fixture amendments. Eva Olid’s women’s side drew 1-1 at Arsenal on 19 September and are still waiting on a first WSL win of the season.

Where sources agree — and disagree

The agreement is broad and it is not flattering. Sky Sports, Al Jazeera, Goal and the club’s own match coverage report the same facts: 2-3 to Brighton at home from 2-0 up, 1-1 at Fulham, five points from five, twelfth, joint-worst Premier League start. Nobody is disputing the scoreline, the sequence or the table. There is also no credible reporting that United have made contact with any replacement manager.

The disagreement is about cause, not fact. Carrick attributes it to margins: “It’s pretty fine margins that have flipped it… I’m not despondent because I think we’ve shown some good signs as well,” adding that United are “having a spell that feels bigger than it actually is to the outside.” Neville attributes it to application and structure — effort out of possession, and a squad built of players “better with the ball than without the ball.” Mick Brown, the former United chief scout, told Football Insider the fault lies with squad weaknesses rather than the manager. Three explanations, one set of results. This comes as some voices openly questioned tactics, substitutions and whether or not United were executing a plan in Wednesday’s defeat to Brighton, or whether the squad is physically unfit to apply consistent pressure to an opposition for extended periods of a game.

The disagreement about Carrick’s security is about sourcing, not substance. TEAMtalk, as relayed by Football365, reports that INEOS has decided against a change and that “nobody at United has had contact with Conte or any other potential managerial candidate.” Football Insider, citing Brown, says Carrick “has the full backing of the Man United board” while conceding he is “certainly under pressure.” Neville’s framing is compatible with both: “no one is putting pressure on Carrick – but if those types of performances continue, he will find himself in a lot of trouble.” What none of these sources offer is a first-party club statement, and the candidate names circulating — Howe, Conte, Hürzeler, McKenna, Nagelsmann — are speculation, not reporting. Treat them accordingly.

What remains unknown: how long Šeško is out, whether Rashford’s hip problem is a short absence or a managed condition for the season, and whether the hierarchy’s current position survives a bad October.

What it means

United have scored in every league game and put five past Ipswich and four past Sabah in the Champions League, so this is not a side that cannot create. The Fulham numbers — two efforts off the woodwork, several Leno saves — support Carrick’s claim that the chances exist. The problem is at the other end and in the middle of games: a 2-0 lead surrendered at home, a lead conceded to a bottom-half side, and a second half at Craven Cottage that even the goalscorer described as short of energy.

The timing of the break cuts both ways. It gives Šeško roughly three weeks to be fit for Tottenham, gives de Ligt and Baleba time, and gives Carrick a fortnight of coaching without a fixture — useful if the diagnosis really is defensive structure. It also gives three weeks of talk with no result to change the subject, which is the part of an international break no struggling manager wants.

THE UNITED ROUNDUP VIEW

The same failure twice

The encouraging part is real and it is being underweighted: United create chances, the front line is not short of quality, and a fit Šeško changes the shape of the attack. The concern is that the failures this week were not about finishing. Losing from 2-0 up at home and being out-worked by a winless Fulham are the same failure twice, and “fine margins” does not describe either. Carrick is entitled to his fortnight, and the reporting that the board is not minded to act looks credible — but the standard has now been set by his own words. He said United will “get back to winning football matches.” Tottenham at home on 10 October is where that claim gets tested.

What would change our assessment: a genuinely controlled defensive performance against Spurs, particularly in the period after United go in front, assuming they do, of course.

What would harden it: another lead surrendered.

What comes next

United’s next fixture is Tottenham at Old Trafford on Saturday 10 October, followed by Atlético Madrid in the Champions League on 13 October. Šeško’s test results this week are the thing to watch, along with whether Rashford’s hip issue is resolved during the break. In the diary for November: Aston Villa at home on Sunday 8 November, 16:30, and Liverpool away on Sunday 22 November, 16:30.
