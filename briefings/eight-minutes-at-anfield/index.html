Eight Minutes at Anfield

Four days, one training session, a drone and an £18.1m loss, then a red card and 4-0. Hardly anyone blames Pochettino. Plenty blame the people who hired him, and Keane blames the players.

A new head coach, a £7.58m fee, another £40m of borrowing and a club nineteenth in the table. Then Anfield. How much more can one week break?

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · Monday 23 November 2026

Right, where to start. Mauricio Pochettino's first game as United head coach was a contest for about eight minutes. Leny Yoro was sent off for bringing down Alexander Isak, Liverpool were two up by the thirteenth minute, and it finished 4-0 at Anfield [12]. United are 19th with eight points [13] and have conceded 34 goals in all competitions, the worst record in Europe's top five leagues [30]. Thirteen managers had taken a side to Anfield for their first league game in charge before him, and twelve had lost [11]. Make that thirteen out of fourteen.

And Sunday was only the end of it. He landed on Wednesday, and by Thursday evening a drone had filmed twelve minutes of his first training session and put it on YouTube [1][2]. While he was telling the in-house cameras that "the culture is the most important" [3], the club published first-quarter accounts showing the net loss nearly tripling to £18.1m and long-term debt past £577m [4]. Four fans were banned over the tennis balls thrown on during the Roma game [5][6], JJ Gabriel posted his own farewell [7], Bruno Fernandes was reported to have turned down another contract offer [8], and Juan Mata retired [9].

On Friday a long Daily Mail investigation into co-owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe landed, built around a sailor's court case that has nothing to do with football but plenty to do with how the people at the top of United operate. In it, a United board member is alleged to have told Sir Ben Ainslie that if you don't give Jim what he wants, "he will burn your house down" (Ineos says it was a figure of speech) [10]. The same day, chief executive Omar Berrada said United had nine defenders to choose from and the summer market offered no "value" to improve the defence [30]. Seven of those nine played at Anfield [30]. This is where we're at.

The week at a glance

Pochettino in. United confirmed Pochettino as head coach on 13 November, on a deal to June 2029 [14][15]. The Athletic traces the process back to late October and a $10m buyout clause in his US Soccer contract; Unai Emery, Fabian Hürzeler and Cesc Fàbregas were all discussed, with the Glazers described as driving the final call [16]. He flew in on Wednesday with assistant Jesus Pérez and goalkeeping coach Toni Jiménez [17]. The Telegraph counted seven uses of the word culture in his first club interview [18]; by Friday's press conference he was saying "we are not focused on culture because it's a massive word, culture" [19].

The drone. Roughly twelve minutes of Thursday's session, filmed from a drone, went up on YouTube under the account "CC Overview" and passed 60,000 views within a day [1][2]. United have not commented. The uploader says the airspace is unrestricted and the operator holds the relevant CAA licences, adding that "it certainly won't be the only footage of training released" [2]. He has since apologised, according to a post shared by the fan channel United Peoples TV on 20 November [56].

Liverpool 4-0 United. Pochettino made three changes from the 4-4 with Villa: Matthijs de Ligt made his first Premier League start since November 2025, Lisandro Martinez returned from suspension and Patrick Dorgu came in, with Maguire, Mazraoui and Amad on the bench [20]. Before kick-off he told MUTV he was "very sure" that XI could perform [21]. In the eighth minute De Ligt let Ronald Araujo's long ball go over him, and Yoro was shown a straight red for a last-man foul on Alexander Isak, judged to have denied an obvious goalscoring opportunity [12][22]. Dominik Szoboszlai scored the free-kick and Florian Wirtz made it 2-0 three minutes later. Maguire replaced Mbeumo after 22 minutes and Joshua Zirkzee came on for Matheus Cunha at half-time; Rio Ngumoha (66) and Victor Munoz (82) finished it [12]. The club notes Pochettino is the first United boss to start his reign against Liverpool [12]. The Daily Mail's Oliver Holt calls it the quickest sending-off in United's Premier League history and the worst result in a United manager's first game for more than 100 years [23]. Pochettino: "we play more than 80 minutes with 10 men. It's unfair." He also said "I take responsibility for today" [24], and told the players to "keep the head up and translate that anger to an energy" [25].

The Q1 accounts. Revenue rose 4.1% to £146.1m, but the net loss went from £6.6m to £18.1m as profit on player sales fell from £45m to £20.7m [4]. Long-term borrowings rose from $650m to $775m, about £577.4m, mostly down to June's refinancing [26][4]. Simon Stone says United have borrowed another £40m since 30 September, in three chunks, the latest £10m on 16 November [57]. Omar Berrada's results statement: "Our revenue remains strong and on track, while we continue to grow sustainably." [27] The Daily Mail's Chris Wheeler adds that wages rose 25% on Champions League qualification, and reminds readers that co-owner Avram Glazer recently said the club's value "is not dependent on success on the pitch" [28].

What the change cost. Michael Carrick was sacked on 13 November [29]. Wheeler puts the combined bill at £15m: £10m to US Soccer for Pochettino and his staff, and £5m to pay off Carrick and his [28]. Simon Stone says the club have since confirmed a fee of £7.58m payable for Pochettino's appointment [57].

Berrada speaks. On Friday the chief executive said United had nine defenders to pick from and there was no "value" in the summer market to improve the defence, according to the Manchester Evening News's Tyrone Marshall [30]. Asked about the Premier League case against Manchester City, where he held finance roles before joining United, he said: "I wasn't involved in any wrongdoing. I wasn't involved in the proceedings. There are no allegations against me." City have appealed the verdict [31].

Fans banned. Four supporters have been banned from Old Trafford over the tennis balls thrown on during the Champions League win over Roma on 3 November, a protest aimed at the Glazers; at least one ban is for three years, the maximum for "missile throwing" [5][6].

Fernandes. The captain has turned down a second contract offer [8]. His deal runs to 2027 with a one-year club option, which BBC Sport's Simon Stone says United are "almost certain" to trigger [32]. On Friday Pochettino said: "Bruno is our captain and is a very important player," and left the contract for another day [19].

JJ Gabriel. The sixteen-year-old forward has signed with Ali Barat's Epic Sports agency and posted his own farewell, signed off "Thank you for everything. JJ" [33][7]. Fabrizio Romano says the Real Madrid move is "at the final points" [34]. Football365, citing ESPN, reports United still hold his registration and are "ready to take legal action against Real Madrid or any other club that illegally approached" him [35][36].

The first press conference. Pochettino said he'd spent three hours in individual meetings with the squad in his office on Thursday [19][37]. The Manchester Evening News, citing the i Paper, reports a full squad audit is high on his list, and that he has previously sorted players into good, excellent and world-class bands [38]. He confirmed he picks the matchday squad, and pushed back on judging the squad's running data without context [39].

Elsewhere. January: Romano names centre-back and striker as the priorities [40], while the Daily Mail's Confidential column calls the budget "modest", with loans considered [41]. Juan Mata has retired at 38 after 285 appearances and 51 goals for United [9][42]. Chido Obi's loan at Willem II has produced no goals in two months, and a January recall is being considered [43]. Senne Lammens saved a stoppage-time penalty as Belgium beat Italy 4-1 [29].

What’s been confirmed

  • Carrick was sacked and Pochettino appointed head coach, on a contract to June 2029, on 13 November [14][15][29].
  • Liverpool 4-0 United, with Yoro shown a straight red in the eighth minute for denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity [12]. The Premier League Match Centre says VAR checked and confirmed it [13].
  • United's Q1 figures: revenue £146.1m, net loss £18.1m, operating loss £3m, long-term borrowings $775m (about £577.4m) [26][4].
  • Four supporters have been banned over the Roma protest [5][6].
  • A drone filmed Pochettino's first session and the footage was published on YouTube. United have not commented [1][2].
  • Fernandes stays captain [19]. JJ Gabriel has signed with Epic Sports and remains registered to United [33][35][7].

Who’s saying what: who answers for the club above Pochettino?

Little of the coverage blames Pochettino for Sunday, beyond Neville saying he was "absolutely stunned" to see De Ligt's name on the team sheet [22]. The argument is about who sits above him, and after Anfield a second argument has started about the players.

Not much has been divulged about how the managerial change was handled internally. Ratcliffe and Wilcox have said nothing publicly on either the sacking or the appointment. Berrada's on-record comments are the results statement and Friday's interviews [27][30][31]. Pochettino, asked about the hiring process, declined to go into it [39].

Who is saying what:

  • The Manchester United Supporters' Trust: responsibility for United's problems "do not primarily rest with him or indeed his predecessors as manager. They lie with the ownership." [44][45]
  • Gary Neville, on Stick To United before the game, called the way Carrick's exit was handled "disgraceful" and said he would be "very surprised" if Wilcox weren't moved back to his previous, more technical role, with a sporting director brought in to handle recruitment and manager selection [46].
  • Tyrone Marshall (MEN) says Pochettino "will rightly avoid scrutiny" and "sights will be trained on those above him", pointing out that United have now conceded 34 goals in all competitions, the worst record in Europe's top five leagues, two days after Berrada's comments on the defence [30].
  • Roy Keane, on Sky Sports, turned it round: "We're blaming the board, blaming ownership, blaming managers. When do the players take responsibility for some decision-making?" [13]
  • Kobbie Mainoo, to MUTV, answered for the dressing room: "I can't fault the boys on the fight" and "No one's going anywhere" [47].
  • The Athletic reports the Glazers were "exerting more influence" on the appointment; United call claims of an ownership split "categorically false" [16].
  • The Daily Mail's Ian Herbert reports Ainslie's allegations from the now-settled America's Cup case, including board member Rob Nevin's "scorched earth" remark. Ineos denies false imprisonment and says the remark was figurative. Herbert's unnamed sources describe a "cult-like" culture around Ratcliffe at United [10].
  • The Telegraph's Jamie Carragher, before the game, thought United had hired a man with "a big reputation, but not enough on his CV to back up glowing testimonials" [48].

The Ratcliffe reporting doesn't concern the football club in a legal sense; the court case was about a yacht. Holt noted Berrada and Wilcox in the directors' box at Anfield, a row in front of Sir Alex Ferguson [23].

Where sources agree and disagree

Agree. Nobody disputes the appointment, the sacking or the scoreline. The Q1 figures match to the pound across Sky Sports News, dave.sport and the BBC, MEN and Sun journalists working from the same release [26][4][49][50][51]. Everyone who watched Anfield agrees the defending before the red card was poor, including those who thought Yoro was hard done by: Keane called it "really poor defending" even while saying Isak wasn't getting the ball [52], and Neville called it "unforgivable" [22].

Disagree.

  • The red card. The referee and VAR gave and confirmed it for denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity [13]. Pochettino said "the ball was for the goalkeeper", Kobbie Mainoo said "No chance" [52], Keane said Isak was not getting the ball [52], and Marshall thought it "looked harsh, with Isak initiating the contact" [30]. Holt went the other way: "It was not a controversy." [23] Pochettino also says Jeremy Jacquet's challenge on Mbeumo moments earlier was a penalty [24][25].
  • Picking De Ligt. Neville was "absolutely stunned" to see his name on the team sheet [22], and Holt called it "Not the best selection decision by the new boss" [23]. Pochettino's answer: "He was training, he played in the Champions League before the international duty." [52]
  • Was 4-0 "not too bad"? Carragher said United "did okay considering the situation they found themselves in", and Neville said the travelling fans "deserve so much better". Keane accused both of going easy: "I feel like swearing." [13]
  • A relegation fight? Holt says yes: "This is a relegation fight that United are in. Don't kid yourself that it's not." [23] On Sky's post-match panel the view was split, with one pundit saying he didn't believe United were in one and expected them to "come out of it" [55]. Pochettino: "There are still a lot of games to play." [24]
  • The bans. Yalla Shoot says four fans were banned, "at least one" for three years [5]; Brit Brief mentions only "at least one supporter" [6]; Holt writes that all four were banned for three years [23].
  • The fee, still open from earlier in the week. Wheeler's £10m for Pochettino's side [28] against The Athletic's $10m buyout plus "over $2 million more" for staff [53]. Simon Stone now says the club have confirmed a fee of £7.58m payable for Pochettino's appointment [57], lower than both, and it isn't clear whether that figure covers his staff.
  • JJ Gabriel, also still open. The Peoples Person frames the move as close, with a £300,000 compensation figure discussed [33]; Football365, via ESPN, frames it as adversarial, with United threatening legal action [35][36].

The United Roundup view

Opinion

Well, the appointment itself looks defensible — Pochettino's a sensible, experienced hire for a club that's run out of better options — but the week's other stories all point to so many other issues the club is facing and most of these problems are the same problem in different clothes. Look, you can't fault the timing. United waited until their new head coach had been in the building for about six hours before the drone footage, the loss figures and the fan bans all came in the same week. Then you give him Liverpool away as his first game in charge and only three or four days to prepare. If you wanted to design a stress test for a bloke who's just left international football for a club job, this is roughly it.

The agree/disagree sections above show some reasonably solid reporting across the board this week, with a few exceptions that appear genuinely murky as opposed to anyone getting it wrong.

The Liverpool game went about as well as you would expect, pretty much on par with the absolute mess that is Manchester United Football Club right now. 4-0 was a beating but it could easily have been 6,7,8….who knows! Yes, we were up against it after Yoro clattered into the back of Alexander Isak and was sent off after just eight minutes. Senne Lammens was to thank for some good saves and was probably our best player (which says a lot when you lose 4-0) but he was also arguably at fault for setting up the wall for Liverpool's freekick that Dominik Szoboszlai smashed through straight to score Liverpool's first. It’s hard not to feel a bit bad for the Yoro because there is an argument that Isak took a heavy touch and wasn’t going to get to the ball before the keeper and was therefore looking for the contact and a freekick, a tactic we see way too much of in the modern game, but the referee made an on the field decision and didn’t seek VAR help so it stood as a straight red card.

I’m not going to sit here and say that even if we hadn’t been a man down, we would’ve fared any better for the rest of the game. Liverpool looked clinical and showed no mercy - more energetic, more organised and way more cohesive as a unit. United on the other hand, showcased the same issues we’ve been pointing out all season. Sloppy defending, unorganised in attack and lacking quality. On and off the ball looked way behind the pace of what a premier league team should, bad decision making, slow to react, and never really looked like they were going to get anything out of the game, even with the few chances they did have.

The team selection was the first thing that struck me as odd - Matthijs De Ligt was picked ahead of Harry Maguire in the starting line up and was promptly substituted after just twenty-two minutes after a rusty performance, no surprise really considering it was his first start in nearly a year. For me, Liverpool away was not the place to try him out. I’m asking myself, what is going on in training that we don’t see on the pitch? Because we rarely see a lineup and think, yes, that’s our best eleven. And substitutions are still leaving us scratching our heads. So how does a manager deal with all that while trying to get his philosophy across to the squad in such a busy fixture period? Personally, I think they need more time and multiple transfer windows and a clear long term plan…..at this stage I think we need to stop worrying about results and concentrate on building a squad of substantial depth and quality.

The mess sitting above the manager - the ownership structure, debt, accountability, an aging squad - some of which are coming to the end of their contracts, a tired and angry fanbase are the other stories, and Pochettino is just the latest person stood in front of it all. Anyone in football with at least one eye and one ear could tell you that the manager alone at Manchester United is nothing short of a fall guy if he’s not given the tools and squad to achieve his visions. So will Mauricio be the latest in a nauseatingly long line of casualties since Sir Alex retired? Will he be given meaniful backing in the transfer window? And by backing, I don’t just mean the all important budget, I mean to what extent will his say be heard in the conversation around which players stay, go or join? There isn’t much anyone can say with words that will convince the fanbase that they can safely dare to hope for success at the club. Like I said, we’ve heard this song before. United supporters know that no meaningful revival back to anything resembling United’s former glories can be even contemplated while the current ownership, debt and hierarchical chain of command is still in place.

The Ratcliffe/Ineos reports that emerged late this week shines a slightly more concerning light over it — it's not about United specifically, but it's hard to read the Ainslie allegations and not wonder what "scorched earth" management looks like applied to a football club rather than a sailing team. And is it to blame for some of the decisions that we’ve all seen being made since Sir Jim Ratcliffe came in?

The drone leak doesn’t really bother me too much. The uploader (CC Overview) has apologised and removed the video. It is an opportunity for rivals and talking heads to insinuate security flaws or even suggest that Pochettino training and tactical secrets were on show. I don’t think that a few minutes of positional play with dummies and cones tells you much beyond a possible formational set up. I think any opposition manager worth his salt will see it and certainly recognise what is happening but won’t think they have an upper hand or glean any major insight from the footage alone. As for security,I’m not sure what the club can actually do apart from applying to increase the no fly zone for drones around the facility. I still don’t think that stops today's cameras. All-in-all, a non-story but bad timing.

The finances concern me way more than a drone does. My fear is that even if United are able to somehow bring some of next summer's player budget forward to the January transfer window that it will ultimately not be enough to plug the gaps and will have a knock on effect for the next transfer window budget. I also know that it will take more than a couple of transfer windows in the club and squads current state to fix much of what needs attention.

The fans banned for legitimate protest by some reports received multi-year bans for throwing projectiles onto the pitch. Harsh in my view but we can expect anyone to encourage chucking stuff in the pitch during a game so it was probably what you’d expect. A deterrent more than anything.Again the fans suffer for showing their frustration towards the ownership for their treatment of fans, price hikes and disregard for the clubs history.

JJ Gabriel might actually be a nice lad if testimonies from within the club are to be believed. But we can assume that the advice he is being given about how to market himself or respond to not being fast tracked into the United first team setup isn’t far short of confrontational and arrogant. His Epic Sports promo was an absolute cringeworthy AI slopfest in my opinion and a product of the typically wrong influences and marketing styles that his generation seem to gravitate toward nowadays. It wreaks of a lack of self awareness and immaturity. The subsequent farewell video crediting United for his development and thanking them for everything was too little too late and probably largely fell on deaf ears.

Supporting United is like owning a car with a knackered engine, bald tyres and a loan taken out against it, and the owners’ answer is to change the driver every couple of years and act surprised when it still won’t get up the hill.

It’s worth being honest about how low the bar was for Sunday. Any result in week one of a new manager really isn’t the end of the world, even if it was a convincing defeat at Anfield. Unfortunately, Manchester United fans are all too conditioned to the kinds of events transpiring around the club at present, which isn’t a good thing. They should be, and deserve to be genuinely excited by a new managerial appointment after everything their club has put them through over the last decade-plus, and be feeling excited by his plans. At least, that’s how it’s supposed to go, and maybe it did for the first few head coach appointments post Ferguson. As much as the fans will support Pochettino and may like him as a person, it’s hard to be taken in….again.

Every bit of good news, a player back from injury, a youngster coming through, a penalty save for Belgium, an international goal is like finding a fiver in your coat pocket on the day the bailiffs come round. Nice, but it’s not changing anything.

What happens next

  • Sporting away on Wednesday, then Brentford at home on Saturday [54], the start of a run Sky Sports put at 10 games in 39 days [55]. Holt says another week like this puts United in the bottom three in December for the first time in more than 50 years [23].
  • The defence: whether De Ligt keeps his place after Sunday, and whether Pochettino asks for defenders in January after Berrada's line that there was no "value" in the summer market [30][41].
  • The audit: whether the squad review the MEN describes shows up in Pochettino's next team sheets [38].
  • Fernandes: Pochettino put the contract question off on Friday, and BBC Sport expects United to trigger the extra year [19][32].
  • Accountability: whether Ratcliffe, Berrada or Wilcox say anything after Anfield [30].
  • The drone: the uploader first said more footage would follow [2], then apologised [56]. Whether United take it any further is still open.
