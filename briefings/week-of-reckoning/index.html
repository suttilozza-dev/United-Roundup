Carrick stays, Fernandes returns, no striker: who's saying what about United's week of reckoning?

Plus Who's Saying What: why Carrick was kept, and who owns the blame

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · Saturday 31 October 2026 (covering 26 October – 1 November)

Michael Carrick left the Old Trafford pitch on Sunday teary-eyed, waving to the Stretford End in what looked to plenty of people like a farewell lap. By Tuesday lunchtime he had been at Carrington since 7.30am, the club had let it be known that he would lead United at Chelsea on Saturday, but nobody at the top of the club had said a word in public about why.

Nobody disputes how bad it has been: one league win in eight, 16th place, two heavy defeats in five days. What is disputed is almost everything else. Why was he kept? Was that right? Is Bruno Fernandes's public criticism of his head coach a one-off or a symptom? Who owns the blame? And who comes next if Saturday goes the same way?

It was a big week, so this is a bigger piece than usual. Rather than pick one quote per argument, we have set out who said what on each talking point, labelled every claim, and pulled the real disagreements together in one place. Numbers in square brackets point to the sources at the bottom.

Who is saying what?

Eight key talking points from the week, ordered chronologically with key claims and named sources.

1. How Carrick survived: the 72 hours

After the Bournemouth defeat it took nearly 44 hours, by The Athletic's count, for Carrick's position to be made clear [4]. Berrada and Wilcox spoke to him briefly after the game and were at Carrington on Monday while he watched the Under-21s play Hull [5]. No executive has explained the decision on the record. What exists is briefing, and it points in several directions at once.

2. Fernandes: a reset, or a symptom?

After the 4-1 defeat at Como, Fernandes told TNT Sports: “I think sometimes we have to be more brave under pressure” [18]. Asked whether Carrick had got it wrong tactically, the Mail reports he replied: “That's on the manager and staff to see it, not me” [20]. Carrick dropped him for Bournemouth and kept him on the bench while four other substitutes came on [11]. Fernandes later liked an Instagram post reading “Never push a loyal person to the point where they no longer care” [3]. The two have since spoken.

Worth noticing

Almost every source, including the sympathetic ones, thinks saying it in public was a mistake. The split is over what it means. Tielemans and the club figures treat it as frustration already resolved. Neville says it is “only 1 or 2% of what they're actually saying internally” [24], which is inference: no named person has given an on-the-record account of what is being said inside the dressing room. On the sanction, Nevin thinks Carrick got it right; Neville wants him either used or left out. And the Instagram “like” is a reading, not a statement: Fernandes says nothing about it in any source here.

Worth noticing
  • Keane and Neville both doubted Carrick as a long-term appointment, and both welcomed him getting more time. Neville still thinks the club decided badly. That is not a clean sack-him camp.
  • Claims about how Carrick feels, and what the players think of him, are inference. Carragher says Carrick would want to be put out of his misery and that he “can never say publicly” [33][24]. Carrick's own on-the-record words are that he is “desperate to make us better and improve things” [7]. Maguire and Tielemans back him publicly; Sutton calls it “clearly not a happy and harmonious dressing room” [25]; 48.4% of Athletic respondents doubt the players will get behind him [9]. None of that can be checked against what is said in private.
  • On the applause: Andy Mitten says fans know “all the problems are not on him” [10]. The Telegraph says the singing did not go unnoticed inside the club, but others read it as supporters pointing the blame elsewhere [11]. Sutton does not “necessarily think that has bought him time” [25].

Worth noticing

Where sources agree — and disagree

Common ground

  • The record is not in dispute: 7 points from 8, 16th, eight without a win, two heavy defeats in five days. The players say so themselves [1][27].
  • The reprieve is conditional. The BBC, The Athletic, the Telegraph and the Mail all describe a chance to turn things round with no stated timescale [3][12][11][5].
  • The recent performances were not good enough. Carrick, Maguire, Stone, Neville and Rooney differ on why, not on that [35][27][34][17][23].
  • Fernandes saying it in public was a mistake, and he and Carrick have since spoken [19][20][6].
  • Fans separated Carrick from the players and the owners on Sunday; the BBC, The Athletic and the Telegraph all describe the same split [2][10][11].
  • Sesko is out for a long time and there is no like-for-like cover [8][51].

The United Roundup view

Opinion

The reprieve is a decision with no reason on the record. What we know about why Carrick stayed comes from people who haven't put their names to it, and the explanations on offer (cost, no agreed replacement, the timing of the break, a hope the players respond) are reasons for a delay, not a plan. Nobody has set a timescale. Supporters are entitled to ask the people who made the decision to explain it in their own names, and to notice that the only person facing questions week after week is the one whose job is on the line.

The evidence supports a few firm conclusions. A seven point return from eight league games and eight matches without a win is poor by any standard, and this week's two heavy defeats were worse than the results alone suggest. On who is responsible there are at least three honest answers: the players, the head coach, and the people who built the squad and set the budget. Nothing in this piece lets anyone claim one of them alone. The squad, the recruitment, and the coaching strategy can all be failing at once.

What can't be claimed as fact is most of what was shouted loudest. Nobody has shown that the players have stopped trying for Carrick, that he wants to be sacked, that Fernandes was leading anything, or that the owners are feuding the way anonymous sources describe. Claims about what is going on inside Carrington are inference from people who aren't in the room. The money figures, as the table shows, don't agree with each other, and “cost” is a reason nobody at the club has given. I'd want a named person to say it before treating it as the reason.

What would change this assessment isn't a single result. A win at Chelsea doesn't fix a team that is 16th, and a defeat doesn't prove it can't be fixed. Before the international break I'd want to see performances that look like a plan rather than a reaction, a team that plays as a unit, and someone at the top of the club saying something in public. If none of that happens, the reprieve was a postponement, and the questions about who picked the squad and who set the budget deserve the same airtime as the ones about the head coach.
