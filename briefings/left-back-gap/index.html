WEEKLY ROUNDUP

United come out of the break with a different injury list, and the same hole at left-back

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · 5 October 2026 · covering 28 September – 4 October 2026

Manchester United haven't played since the 1-1 draw at Fulham on 20 September, and sit 12th with five points from five, their joint-lowest return after five games in the Premier League era (Confirmed). The fortnight off turned out to be a swap rather than a rest: six players picked up or carried problems during it, with no club diagnosis for any of them, while five others are expected back for Tottenham. What matters is where each group sits. The new problems are in the one position United cannot cover; the returns are where they already had bodies. Tottenham, Atlético Madrid and Leeds follow inside nine days.

Now, with the Premier League soon to resume, fans are looking at the aftermath of an international break that has seen some promising signs from some United players while seeing returning names being replaced on the injury list with new concerns. Carlos Baleba is a name on a lot of lips as he seeks his Premier League debut for the reds. While other older concerns still seem to be rearing their ugly head, like the left-back position, which seems to be an elephant in the room lately when it comes to the Manchester United squad.

The leading story

Patrick Dorgu's withdrawal is confirmed. Its nature however, is not. He came off roughly 30 minutes into Denmark's 2-0 win over Wales on 27 September after getting hurt off the ball, and was sent back to Manchester. Denmark coach Brian Riemer: "He has an injury and has to go home." (Reported — The Star, wire report, 28 September.) He has since been at Carrington for tests, with nothing published since (Reported — Football Insider, via Yahoo Sports, 3 October).

What the injury is depends on who you read, and it is two against two. The wire copy and the Manchester Evening News log a thigh problem; Sports Illustrated and Football Insider describe a hamstring, the latter a brand-new one. Goal declined to name it, stating that "the exact nature and severity of the injury are yet to be officially confirmed". No club or federation diagnosis has been published, so every version is Reported and none Confirmed. Dorgu missed 12 games last season with a hamstring problem (Reported).

The context is the problem, and it is not new. United did not sign a left-back in the summer, leaving 31-year-old Luke Shaw the only senior natural option and Dorgu covering the position. The makeshift answer was Diogo Dalot or Noussair Mazraoui — and Mazraoui was himself substituted during Morocco's 2-0 win over Lesotho on 30 September after a fall, holding his back, with no update since (Reported — FourFourTwo, via Yahoo Sports).

A footnote with a figure on it. Stefan Borson, a former Manchester City financial adviser, told Football Insider that FIFA's club protection scheme would underwrite Dorgu's wages — around £106,000 a week, on his estimate — if the player misses 28 consecutive days or more. Borson's own verdict: it "might make the accountants feel a bit better", but these injuries are "a hammer blow". (Attributed, via Yahoo Sports, 3 October. Nobody has said how long Dorgu is out, so the threshold is hypothetical.)

Joshua Zirkzee gave Michael Carrick a reason to not overlook him as an option up front by netting for the Netherlands in their 2-1 win against Serbia, putting him closer to selection over another injury concern in Benjamin Šeško (Reported — United In Focus, 5 October).

Bruno Fernandes looked like the second worry but seems to have "walked it off" as they say, and is no longer one. He was kept out against Norway as a precaution, with Jorge Jesus calling it "a complicated problem" and saying Bruno had been "playing with that pain for the last three games" (Reported — Al Jazeera, 27 September), he then played 89 minutes against Denmark on 1 October and got two assists (Reported). Something else to note is that Jorge Jesus has been utilising his 'other talisman' (after Cristiano Ronaldo) in a different position to that he plays in a United shirt. Bruno has found himself being made effective from a kind of left wing position as Jesus sets up his team in a slightly unorthodox 4-1-3-2 in some circumstances. Food for thought for Carrick if he wants to forego the likes of Marcus Rashford out left for something new this season.

Benjamin Šeško was withdrawn from the Slovenia squad with the recurring shin problem from the end of last season. The Slovenia camp, as relayed, called it "an atypical injury that requires time to heal" (Reported — Last Word on Sports, 29 September; a relayed quote, not cross-checked against a primary Slovenian source). Šeško posted that "the most important thing is to focus fully on my recovery" (Attributed — his own Instagram statement, via the Manchester Evening News). No return date from anyone.

What is coming back. The Manchester Evening News lists five possible returns for Tottenham — Carlos Baleba, Amad, Kobbie Mainoo, Marcus Rashford and goalkeeper Karl Darlow — but those are the paper's projections, not club statements (Reported). Baleba, still without a debut since his reported £70m move from Brighton, played a behind-closed-doors friendly against Lausanne-Sport at Carrington on Friday (Confirmed — club, 2 October). On Amad, out since the eve of the Hull opener, Carrick is quoted saying "hopefully, by the time we get back, he will have trained with the group"; on Matthijs de Ligt he gave no date, only that "Matta is just working his way back" (Attributed, both via the MEN). Manuel Ugarte and Tom Heaton stay out.

Matthijs de Ligt is nearing a competitive return from a long-term back injury after recently stepping up his training with Manchester United. Some reports say he played in a closed-door friendly against Lausanne-Sport at Carrington on Friday, which the club website has not confirmed (Reported — Stretty News, via Yahoo Sports, 3 October). Manchester Evening News journalist Steven Railston posted to X saying "If Matthijs de Ligt came through yesterday's behind-closed-doors friendly feeling fine, he could make the squad against Tottenham" (Attributed — Steven Railston; the same line is carried by Stretty News).

The other key developments

The argument about Carrick got quieter in substance and louder in tone. Fabrizio Romano reported no approach to alternative coaches and said "the large majority of the players are absolutely backing and supporting the ideas of Michael Carrick" (Attributed — via TeamTalk, 28 September). Sky's Alan Smith put a condition on it: a home defeat to Tottenham "would just increase the pressure" (Attributed — via Read Man Utd, 1 October). The side that condition rests on arrive at Old Trafford bottom of the table themselves, under Roberto De Zerbi, and needing a result of their own (Reported — MEN, 5 October). Jamie Carragher went the other way, calling it "ludicrous to get rid of Michael Carrick" (Attributed, 27 September). Carrick, despite the punditry and speculation outside Carrington and Old Trafford, said "I'm convinced we'll have a good season."

The left-back gap, reportedly explained. According to Tyrone Marshall at the Manchester Evening News, Carrick and director of football Jason Wilcox were satisfied enough with the squad's attacking options over the summer that they would have sold Joshua Zirkzee to help fund a specialist left-back (Reported — MEN, 4 October). The same piece reports that Marcus Rashford does not want to play through the centre, and names Danny Welbeck as a striker Marshall believes United should have pursued instead (Reported, same piece). On the team's shape, Marshall reports Carrick has deliberately steered United toward patient, possession-based build-up and away from last season's transition-based approach — a change he says "is yet to properly bed in" (Reported — MEN, 4 October).

Mazraoui, worse than feared. Noussair Mazraoui did not make Morocco's matchday squad for their game against Mali, having been substituted holding his back against Lesotho the previous week; it remains unclear whether that is an injury or a precaution (Reported — United In Focus, 4 October). The same piece lists Leny Yoro at full-back and promoting academy left-back Harry Amass among the options United has if Dorgu and Mazraoui are both out for Tottenham (Reported).

What Carrick could change. Marshall suggests three specific switches for Spurs: starting Carlos Baleba alongside Kobbie Mainoo, moving Bryan Mbeumo into a central striking role in place of Matheus Cunha, and giving 19-year-old Shea Lacey a chance on the right if Amad isn't ready (Reported — MEN, 5 October). Marshall also qualifies his own paper's return-date column from two days earlier: Amad "has returned to training but it doesn't appear he played against Lausanne-Sport on Friday and he might not be ready next week" (Reported — MEN, 5 October). This is Marshall's proposal, not confirmed team news; Carrick has given no indication of his starting XI.

JJ Gabriel's departure now looks likely. United will offer the 16-year-old academy winger scholarship terms on the day he turns 16 — the date from which, under his EU passport, he can agree a move to a European club and seek FIFA approval. Marshall reports the club now view an exit as the most likely outcome, tracing the dispute back to Gabriel's camp's frustration at eight pre-season minutes and being left out of the Champions League opener against Sabah (Reported — MEN, 4 October).

The accounts, which we have not covered before. On 23 September — outside this window, and after last week's roundup closed — the club reported record revenue of £677.6m for the year to 30 June 2026, an operating profit of £22.6m and a pre-tax loss of £43.0m (Confirmed — company filing). Omar Berrada called it evidence of "the underlying strength of our business, particularly in a season without European football". Kieran Maguire, on Sky Sports, set the counterweight: gross borrowings around £689m and interest near £70m a year (Attributed).

The women won. United beat Liverpool 1-0 in the WSL on 3 October, Julia Zigiotti scoring early — a second straight league win for Eva Olid's side and a first clean sheet of the season. Senne Lammens and Youri Tielemans featured as Belgium beat Turkey 3-0 (both Confirmed).

A good window for the goalkeeper and the midfielder, at least. Lammens had the kind of break United fans will take: a clean sheet and six saves away to Italy, where he was named Man of the Match (Reported — Flashscore, 25 September), then a string of saves in the narrow loss to France, where Belgium conceded only an 88th-minute winner (Reported — Africa Soccer, 28 September). One outlet had him at 18 saves and a single goal conceded across those first two games alone (Reported — extratimetalk, 29 September). Tielemans played the full 90 minutes against Italy (Reported) and featured in both games that followed, before his two-booking suspension ruled him out of Belgium's final fixture against France on 5 October.

Transfer noise, none of it confirmed. TEAMtalk has United monitoring Crystal Palace's Jean-Philippe Mateta as January cover for Šeško; Gazzetta dello Sport puts €100m on Roma's Manu Koné; Stretty News has Morgan Gibbs-White as a long-term successor to Bruno and Shaw leaving in 2027 — all Reported, none with club or player confirmation. The Telegraph listing United among Eddie Howe's possible next jobs is Speculation. Nothing can happen until January.

Where sources agree — and disagree

Agree. The outlets opened this week agree on two things: an injury list that changed shape during the break, and the one-win-in-five start behind it. On Carrick, the patience reading rests on Romano alone — nobody contradicts it, nobody else independently reports it.

Disagree on fact. One live conflict, and it is evenly split: the wire copy and the Manchester Evening News say thigh, Sports Illustrated and Football Insider say hamstring, Goal declines to name it. United say nothing, so it stays unresolved here. A smaller one: the Manchester Evening News logs Amad's problem as a knock, where last week's reporting had it as an ankle.

Disagree on cause. Sky Sports' mid-September analysis leaned on conditioning, quoting Jamie Redknapp — "They didn't look fit enough" — and noting United rank 18th or 19th for sprints and high-speed runs. Opta's expected-points table points elsewhere: United lead the league for shots and for touches in the opposition box, rank fourth for expected goals, and convert 7.1% of shots, fifth-lowest in the division. On those numbers they should sit fourth on 8.4 points — the league's biggest underperformance.

Disagree on tone. Smith frames the pressure as conditional; supporter media run "crisis" on the same record.

What would settle it. A club medical update on Dorgu — which would also settle whether the FIFA 28-day threshold is anywhere near in play — plus one on Mazraoui, Šeško's scan results, and the five projected returns confirmed by the club rather than by a newspaper's return-date column. Carrick's pre-Tottenham press conference, expected around 8-9 October, is where most of that should land.

What it means

United are creating plenty, finishing badly, and carrying a structural hole that one substitution has exposed. Against that, Opta's numbers are usually a better guide to the next ten games than five results are.

THE UNITED ROUNDUP VIEW — OPINION

The United Roundup view

The left-back gap wasn't an oversight. It was a trade, and United have lost both ends of it. The striker they were willing to sell scored for the Netherlands this week, and the position his sale was meant to fund still has nobody in it: Dorgu covered it, Dorgu's hurt, and his understudy couldn't get on a bench in Morocco. Injuries are a reason for this week. They are not a reason for July.

Bruno finding a role on the left, and de Ligt edging back into training, are the nearest thing to good news in any of it — squad depth that didn't cost a fee. Neither of them plays left-back.

On the manager, the noise is louder than the substance. Nothing in the reporting above has the club moving, and the one hard condition anyone has attached to his position is a game United haven't played yet, against a side currently bottom of the division. Set against that, the underlying numbers say this is a fourth-placed team sitting twelfth, and the change of shape behind those numbers was chosen rather than inflicted. That distinction matters. A side playing badly by accident is a coaching problem. A side playing differently on purpose and not yet playing well is a decision you judge in November, not in the first week of October.

What would change this view: the same creation rate through Tottenham, Atlético, Leeds and Como, and still no wins. If the points haven't arrived by early November, the finishing stops being misfortune and starts being the squad — and the squad isn't a question Carrick answers on his own.

This section is the editor’s personal opinion — not reported fact.

What comes next

Tottenham at Old Trafford on Saturday 10 October, 5.30pm, then Atlético Madrid away on 13 October, Leeds away on 18 October, and Como away in the Champions League — a run of four the MEN groups together as the stretch likely to decide what comes next (Reported — MEN, 5 October). Watch for official word on Dorgu and Mazraoui, Šeško's scan results, and whether Baleba's name is on the teamsheet. Carrick's press conference is expected in the build-up.
