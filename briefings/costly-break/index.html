No match, no respite: Dorgu’s injury caps a costly break for United

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · Monday 28 September 2026 · covering the week to Monday 28 September

Manchester United haven’t played a competitive game since the 1-1 draw at Fulham on 20 September, and so far the international break hasn’t been kind. On paper, a fortnight of training is welcome for a side sitting 12th in the Premier League with five points from five matches, their joint-lowest return after five games in the Premier League era, matching Louis van Gaal’s 2014-15 start (AFP, via Al Jazeera).

Instead, the break has taken players away from Michael Carrick rather than handing them back. The latest went on Saturday, when Patrick Dorgu was withdrawn around the half-hour mark of Denmark’s 2-0 Nations League win over Wales and limped straight off down the tunnel (Goal). He’s coming back to Manchester to be checked. This matters more than one substitution usually would, because of where Dorgu plays and who else is missing. United come out of this break into Tottenham, Atlético Madrid and Leeds inside nine days, with the thinnest area of the squad now thinner still.

The leading story

Dorgu’s withdrawal is confirmed; its severity is not. Sports Illustrated reported a hamstring injury picked up after an overlapping run (SI, 27 September). Goal was more careful, stating that “the exact nature and severity of the injury are yet to be officially confirmed”, with Denmark head coach Brian Riemer expected to update afterwards. As of Monday morning neither United nor the Danish federation had published a diagnosis, so “hamstring” should be read as reported, not established.

The context is the problem. SI notes United did not sign a left-back in the summer, leaving 31-year-old Luke Shaw as the only senior natural option and Dorgu, used further forward at times, covering the position. If both are unavailable, Diogo Dalot or Noussair Mazraoui become the makeshift answer.

Dorgu is not alone. Before the Fulham draw Carrick confirmed Matthijs de Ligt, Amad Diallo and Manuel Ugarte would miss out, while saying of Carlos Baleba and Shaw: “There is a chance on both really. Not totally 100 per cent yet.” Kobbie Mainoo and Marcus Rashford were then among five players to withdraw from the England squad on 21 September.

Bruno Fernandes sat out Portugal’s game in Norway with what Jorge Jesus called a “complicated problem”. Jesus said Bruno had been playing through the pain for about three matches, which might explain a few flat afternoons (Goal). He has stayed with the Portugal squad and Jesus wants him ready for their next game, so for now it is being managed rather than serious.

Benjamin Šeško is the least clear case. He pulled out of the Slovenia squad with the shin problem that bothered him in the spring and is still waiting on test results, after Wayne Rooney, Paul Scholes and Nicky Butt had a go at him for missing the Fulham game. Slovenian outlet Ekipa has described the problem as a chronic condition rather than a straightforward injury, and Planet Nogomet reported an MRI scan in London. United have said nothing publicly, so everything about his timeline is currently second-hand.

The good news: Leny Yoro got his first senior France call-up, replacing Real Madrid’s Ibrahima Konaté, and Joshua Zirkzee was a late addition to the Netherlands squad despite not having started a Premier League game this season. Put together, though, Carrick could be short of his captain, Dorgu and Šeško for Spurs.

The other key developments

The accounts landed, and they cut both ways. On 23 September the club published results for the financial year to 30 June 2026: record revenue of £677.6m, record adjusted EBITDA of £216.4m and an operating profit of £22.6m, against a loss of £47.0m before tax (£43.0m after tax). FY27 guidance is £740m-£760m. Chief executive Omar Berrada called it evidence of “the underlying strength of our business, particularly in a season without European football”. The counterweight, set out by football finance lecturer Kieran Maguire on Sky Sports, is debt: gross borrowings of around £689m plus a transfer balance he puts at £350m-£400m, and interest costs near £70m a year, roughly a tenth of turnover. Maguire also notes that a higher league finish and higher ticket prices did much of the work in reaching the record number.

Carrick’s position became the week’s loudest argument. Gary Neville had already warned on Sky Sports on 14 September that the Fulham game “becomes a real pressure game now”, and has been scathing about United’s play, describing one passage as “the worst 15 minutes I’ve ever seen. Absolutely pathetic.” Jamie Carragher took the other side on The Overlap, saying it would be “ludicrous to get rid of Michael Carrick” while framing him as a stabilising figure who may end up “passing the baton on”. Carrick’s own line has not moved: “I’m convinced we’ll have a good season. We’re having a spell that feels bigger than it actually is to the outside.”

A small disagreement. After Fulham, Matheus Cunha suggested in his post-match interview that the home side put more energy into the early second half. Carrick rejected his interviewer’s suggestion that United looked short of intensity and conditioning for periods of the game: “It didn’t look like that to me. I was quite happy with the way we finished the game,” adding, on conditioning: “So I don’t think that’s an issue, no.”

Quieter club business. Mainoo was named United’s September Player of the Month, a landslide winner by the club’s account, and Shea Lacey took Goal of the Month. Bryan Mbeumo was shortlisted for the Premier League’s EA Sports award.

Also this week

Transfers and recruitment

Nothing happens until January, but the noise has started. All of this is reported rather than confirmed.

  • Manu Koné: Gazzetta dello Sport reports Roma have put a €100m (£86m) price on the midfielder United were linked with in the summer (via Read Man Utd). That reads more like “go away” than “come and get him”.
  • Morgan Gibbs-White: Stretty News reports, citing unnamed sources, that he is being considered as Bruno’s long-term successor, with Bayern Munich also interested. Single-source; treat it as a rumour.
  • Luke Shaw: the same outlet reports he may leave when his contract runs out in 2027. Also single-source.
  • The manager’s job: The Telegraph lists United among Eddie Howe’s possible next jobs. That’s speculation, but after this start it shows where the talk around Carrick is heading.

Youth

United have signed two players from City’s academy in a week: England Under-17 midfielder Karim Cassim, an FA Youth Cup winner with City, on 24 September, and 16-year-old midfielder David Eze on 26 September. Post-Brexit rules limit where academies can sign from, so beating your neighbours to the best local teenagers is now the main market.

  • Noah Ajayi, a 17-year-old forward from last season’s FA Youth Cup final side, has signed his first professional contract.
  • Shea Lacey scored a belter for England’s Under-20s.
  • JJ Gabriel is the one heading the other way. The 15-year-old has told United he won’t sign a contract when he turns 16, and Barcelona and Real Madrid are said to be in the race, although Barcelona publicly insist they’re not interested.

Where sources agree — and disagree

Most of it is settled. Every credible outlet reports the same table position and the same numbers: 12th, five points from five, one win, and a 1-1 draw at Fulham on 20 September in which Lisandro Martínez turned the ball into his own net before Cunha equalised late. The financial figures come from the club’s own filing, and the quotes here are all on the record.

Three gaps remain. First, facts: whether Dorgu’s injury is a hamstring at all, where SI says yes and Goal says it is unconfirmed; and how serious Šeško’s shin problem is, where only Slovenian outlets have offered detail.

Second, cause. Sky Sports’ 17 September analysis leaned on conditioning, quoting Jamie Redknapp (“They didn’t look fit enough”) and noting United rank 18th or 19th in the division for sprints and high-speed runs. Opta’s expected-points table, five days later, points elsewhere: United lead the league for total shots (113) and touches in the opposition box (182), rank fourth for expected goals, and are converting 7.1% of shots, the fifth-lowest rate in the division. On Opta’s numbers United “should” have 8.4 points and sit fourth, the biggest underperformance in the league. Carrick is closer to that reading than to the fitness one.

Third, the manager. Last season’s third-place finish colours the argument: Neville and Carragher are looking at the same five games and reaching opposite conclusions.

One thing we are deliberately leaving out: the left-back names circulating for January, including one in SI’s rumour round-up that traces back to a single social-media account. No reporter of standing has established whether United will sign in January or wait until the summer, and until one does it is not news.

What it means

The uncomfortable reading is that United are creating plenty and finishing badly, and that a squad which cost a great deal (a reported £70m for Baleba alone, per BBC Sport) still has a structural hole that one substitution against Wales has exposed.

The more hopeful reading is that Opta’s numbers are usually a better guide to the next ten games than five results are, and that the 4-0 Champions League win over Sabah and the 5-2 against Ipswich showed what this side looks like when chances go in. The evidence so far suggests a team playing considerably better than 12th, without the finishing or the clean sheets (SI counts one all season) to prove it.

THE UNITED ROUNDUP VIEW

A recruitment failure before a coaching one

The encouraging part is real: the underlying attacking numbers are among the best in the division, and Carrick’s refusal to accept the fitness narrative looks defensible rather than defensive. The concern is equally real, and it is not mainly about the head coach. A club posting record revenue while carrying interest payments near £70m a year went into the season with one senior left-back, and is now one injury away from improvising in a position it has known about for two years. That’s a recruitment failure before it’s a coaching one.

What would change this assessment: United converting at a normal rate through October and climbing, which would show the underlying numbers were telling the truth. If shot volume stays high and the points do not come by early November, the finishing problem is a squad problem, and the questions about Carrick get harder to answer with expected goals.

What comes next

United are home to Tottenham on Saturday 10 October, away to Atlético Madrid on 13 October in the Champions League and away to Leeds on 18 October. Watch for official word on Dorgu, confirmation of Šeško’s scan results, and the Premier League’s September award, normally announced in the first days of October. Carrick’s next press conference is expected in the build-up to the Spurs game, though the club has not yet confirmed a time.
