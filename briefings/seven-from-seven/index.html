United block out the chatter at Elland Road: what a point at Leeds says about Michael Carrick

LS
Laurie SutcliffeFounder and Editor, United Roundup · Monday 19 October 2026 · covering 12 – 18 October 2026

Nobody's going to frame a one-all at Leeds as a great result. Seven points from seven, one league win, and a sixth game without a win in all competitions. There's no polishing that to make it look pretty. But if you'd told me on Tuesday night, with Lisandro Martínez stood in front of an Amazon Prime camera having a go at the people upstairs, that the week would finish with a United side scrapping for each other at Elland Road, I reckon plenty would have taken it.

The Leeds game was anticipated as a real test for Carrick. Depending on which paper you picked up this morning, he passed it, failed it, or should never have sat the exam in the first place. That split is what this week's roundup is about. We've gone through the match reports, the ratings, the columns and the podcasts, and laid out who's backing him, who isn't, and why. Further down, I'll tell you where I stand.

It's a big one this week, so there are tables and quotes to break it up. Dip in where you like. The reporting sections are labelled and balanced. My own view sits in one place only, near the end.

The game

First half

Lammens saved Nico Elvedi's header around the 25th minute, at that point "the only chance of the game" according to Ladyman (Reported) [9]. Mbeumo then headed wide from seven yards on 28 (Reported) [3][5], and James Trafford raced out to stop Amad running through (Reported) [5][10].

Then on 35, Tielemans and Amad worked the ball down the right, Tielemans crossed low and Mbeumo scored (Reported) [3][5][1]. That's four goals in seven Premier League games for Mbeumo, and apart from Fernandes nobody else in the squad has more than one (Reported) [1].

Fernandes was booked for a late challenge on Tarik Muharemovic after a heavy touch, and the MEN says he gave the referee "a barrage of abuse" (Reported) [13]. Leeds' Gabriel Gudmundsson was booked for studs on Santos' calf. Darren Cann told BBC Sport he "could've had no complaints had a red card been shown", and Gary Neville said on Sky: "A bit more force and that's a red" (both Attributed) [16]. Three outlets have Ethan Ampadu fortunate not to be booked for an early challenge on Mbeumo (Reported, opinion) [2][5][9].

Salt called Martínez's first half "the best defensive half of United's season so far" (Attributed) [12], and the Athletic said he "kept his head, timed his tackles well" (Attributed, Cerys Jones) [7]. Neville, on his podcast, singled out Yoro for his work in the air, heading away Bogle's crosses, particularly in the first half (Attributed, opinion) [39].

Martínez off, then the equaliser

Martínez did not come out for the second half. Carrick said "He felt his thigh a little bit" (Attributed) [18]. Mazraoui replaced him. Neville said he saw Martínez holding his thigh in the first half and had no doubt the change was down to injury, not behaviour (Attributed, opinion) [39]. Simon Stone reads the choice of Mazraoui over de Ligt as down to de Ligt's fitness "after almost 11 months out with a back complaint" (Reported, the reporter's reading) [1].

Leeds were level on 51. Mazraoui conceded the corner, lost the first header to Muharemovic, and Calvert-Lewin shook off Mbeumo and finished (Reported) [3][7][9]. Who should have been picking Calvert-Lewin up is a question the reports can't agree on:

Second half and substitutions

Leeds had 69% of the ball in the early part of the second half (Reported) [5]. Ladyman says United went half an hour without a shot after scoring, and that Fernandes' effort on 65 minutes was the first chance of note since the goal (Reported) [9]. In added time United were again lucky to not concede. Lammens punched weakly, Anton Stach's shot went through his legs, and Mazraoui cleared off the line (Reported) [5][9].

The changes: Mazraoui for Martínez (46), Mount for Baleba, and Cunha for Tielemans (84). Darlow, de Ligt, Rashford, Zirkzee, Lacey and Amass stayed on the bench (Reported) [2][3]. Reports can't agree on when Mount came on: 72 minutes (BBC), 73 (Guardian report), 74 (Guardian stats, Daily Mail) or 75 (Guardian live blog) [2][3][5][9].

How the changes were read:

  • Guardian's Michael Butler on Mount for Baleba: "looks like a straight swap, which is surprising". On Cunha for Tielemans: "a positive change" (Attributed) [5].
  • Ian Ladyman in the Daily Mail: "Carrick seemed reluctant to introduce Marcus Rashford or Matheus Cunha. Maybe that was a sign that the United manager valued a point above anything else" (Attributed, opinion) [9].
  • Salt: "Carrick must make better decisions in-game, too, with substitutions here failing to make the side better" (Attributed, opinion) [12].
  • Neville, on Baleba coming off, said he could understand it: Baleba had played the last half hour against Tottenham, then Madrid, then Leeds, three games in a week (Attributed, opinion) [39]. Wheeler read the same change as a verdict on Baleba's display (see below).
  • Marshall noted Lammens "went long with every kick, when previously under Carrick United had tried to play out from the back" (Reported) [13].

How the selection was read

  • Seb Stafford-Bloor wrote that the Baleba-Santos pivot "worked well, helping United cope with Leeds' physicality", and that Carrick "still deserves some praise for some of his selections" (Attributed) [7]. Baleba won 100% of his ground and aerial duels (Reported) [7].
  • Stone wrote that Santos' display "questioned why he had been used so sparingly previously" (Attributed) [2]. Wheeler noted Carrick kept Santos on and took Baleba off, "which says a lot for what the head coach thought of his performance" (Attributed) [10].
  • On Tielemans: Stone has him "more effective with two midfielders behind him" [2], Marshall "his best week as a United player" [13], and Stafford-Bloor called his cross "the best technical moment of the game" [7] (all Attributed).
  • Dalot was the fifth different player to start at left-back this season, and Mbeumo has now played on the right, through the middle and on the left in successive games (Reported, BBC touch map) [1].

What he's working with

The back four. Barney Ronay counted the XI at Elland Road: "four defenders signed under Erik ten Hag, two Ruben Amorim recruits, two from the mist-shrouded age of Solskjær", and wrote that "The third-richest club in the world played a second half with three of its back four out of position" (Attributed, opinion) [6]. Salt described "a right back at left back, a centre-back at right back, and finished with a right back at centre-back…" (Attributed, opinion) [12]. Stone made a related point on 5 October, outside this week's window and used here as background: the first-choice defence of Dalot, Maguire, Martínez and Luke Shaw is "just as it was on the opening day of the 2022-23 season. That is a worry" (Attributed) [30].

The treatment room. Before kick-off, talkSPORT reported Šeško out for about two more months, Patrick Dorgu out until late November with a hamstring problem, and Manuel Ugarte still recovering from knee surgery (Reported) [28]. Luke Shaw and Kobbie Mainoo picked up strains against Tottenham. Carrick told Sky they were "close-ish [to being fit] but not close enough" (Attributed) [5].

The week before it

The Martínez story is covered in full in Friday's Who's Saying What, so here's the short version.

United lost 2-0 at Atlético Madrid on Tuesday 13 October. Pablo Barrios scored after 47 seconds, a Johnny Cardoso goal was ruled out for a Marcos Llorente handball in the build-up, and Ademola Lookman scored on 79 (Reported) [34][35]. Leny Yoro was sent off after a VAR review for denying Jonathan David a clear scoring chance: Sky has the card in the 75th minute, Al Jazeera the 74th (Reported) [34][35]. Clinton Morrison called it "soft" (Attributed) [34]. Sky adds United created four big chances without scoring, their most in a Champions League game since September 2012, and Al Jazeera calls it their worst domestic start in 40 years (Reported) [34][35].

After the game Martínez told Amazon Prime: "now we expect more from the top, from upstairs, to bring in more players to compete", and, on the technical director, "I expect more from our technical director. I have to be honest" (Confirmed, the player's own words) [31][21]. Asked if he expected more summer investment: "Yes, 100 per cent" (Attributed) [31].

Carragher called the comments "awful". Wayne Rooney said Martínez had "maybe gone a bit too far" and that the comments were "a bit disrespectful to his team-mates" (both Attributed) [32]. Martínez then posted a statement saying he spoke "out of anger and frustration" and that "my words reflect how I feel" (Attributed) [33]. Sky reports the statement does not apologise or retract (Reported) [33]. Others describe it as an apology. BBC Sport's Simon Stone writes that Martínez started at Leeds "following his apology for the timing and forum of his attack rather than the words themselves" (Attributed) [1]. Neville said on his podcast that "he's come out and apologized" [39], and the presenter of a Sunday pre-match show said Martínez "has subsequently apologized" (both Attributed) [40]. We have left that disagreement standing; see below. Sky Sports News also reports anger in the boardroom and dressing room, at least one team-mate challenging him on the flight home, no disciplinary decision, and no reported internal pressure on Wilcox (Reported) [33]. talkSPORT reports he was fined (Reported) [18].

The Athletic reported, citing sources close to the player, that the outburst was not primarily about his own contract and reflected frustration building since the League Cup exit to Brighton, and that other players share his view of Wilcox and the summer window. It also says he is "believed to be open to a move to Spain, with Madrid his preferred destination" if he leaves (Reported) [22]. No player has said any of that on the record.

Carrick said after the match he had not seen the interview and could not comment on it, but that there was "disappointment within the group", himself included (Attributed) [34].

Two things stand out. Nobody in the first group says Carrick is the answer. Their argument is that sacking him doesn't fix the problem. Ronay even adds "it is surely worth keeping him just to try, for once, to do something different", while also predicting that "United probably will sack their current manager before the year is out" (Attributed, opinion) [6]. Whitwell goes on: if Carrick goes, "attention will turn to the hierarchy for overseeing a third mid-season change in a row" (Attributed) [25].

Ladyman, meanwhile, argues the problems are "application" more than tactics (Attributed) [9].

Neville, on his podcast, gives Carrick credit for adjusting at Leeds and contrasts that with Amorim, who he says never adjusted (Attributed, opinion) [39]. He also sets a bar: "fourth would be a birdie, fifth would be par and sixth would be a bogey", and "fifth is a must for Michael Carrick to continue to grow". If United are 13th in January, he says, "he's got a problem" (Attributed, opinion) [39]. He argues United have been better sitting deep and countering than being asked to dominate games, under several managers (Attributed, opinion) [39].

From the fan side, the Stretford Paddock post-match review [41], presented by Jay Motty and Stephen Howson, reads the shape as nearer a 4-2-4 that gave United a box in the middle of the park, four against three, which is given as the reason Baleba and Santos were not run over. It is said to have cost United numbers going forward (Attributed to Stretford Paddock, opinion). The show dismisses the "are the players playing for the manager" question, puts the repeated goals from crosses and corners on the players as well as the manager, and says Carrick's first six months are enough to give him the benefit of the doubt, with Fernandes' form and the missing left-back and striker as context (Attributed to Stretford Paddock, opinion). Later a voice on the show argues that Carrick may go regardless, because patience is scarce at United and the same people would choose the next manager (Attributed to Stretford Paddock, opinion). The transcript has no speaker labels, so we credit the show and cannot say whether Motty or Howson said which line.

Where sources agree, and disagree

Agree. United were more committed and compact than against Spurs, and poor after half-time (Guardian, Athletic, Daily Mail, ESPN, MEN). Maguire was their best and Fernandes among their worst (BBC, MEN, Daily Mail). United did not play the listed 4-2-3-1, and defended with three or five without the ball (Guardian, Daily Mail x2, ESPN, MEN). The squad was short at left-back and up front, and the summer spend did not address it (Athletic, Daily Mail, Guardian, Sky, BBC). Martínez came off with a thigh problem, not for disciplinary reasons (talkSPORT, BBC, Guardian, and Neville on his podcast).

Disagree on fact.

  • The cost of the three summer midfielders: £155m (Athletic, MEN) against £163m (Sky).
  • When Mount came on: 72 to 75 minutes depending on the outlet.
  • Who was picking up Calvert-Lewin for the equaliser (table above).
  • Yoro's red card at Atlético: 74th minute (Al Jazeera) or 75th (Sky).
  • Whether Martínez apologised. Sky says his statement "does not apologise" [33]. Stone at the BBC [1], Neville [39] and a Sunday pre-match show presenter [40] all say he did. They may mean different things by the word, and we haven't resolved it.
  • Where United rank on summer spend. The Athletic lists four clubs spending more [21]. A Sunday pre-match show graphic put United 10th, behind Ipswich and Hull too [40].
  • Whether Wilcox is under internal pressure. Sky Sports News reports no internal pressure on him [33]. The Athletic's Whitwell describes Wilcox as trusted by Ratcliffe and close to Berrada, but says "some who have dealt with Wilcox sense" pressure building on him (Reported) [21]. Neither is on the record.

Disagree on reading.

  • The shape: "creative" (Athletic) against "damage-limitation" (ESPN).
  • The shape, again: a box midfield of four against three, nearer a 4-2-4 (Stretford Paddock [41]), against a back three or five without the ball (Jackson, Ladyman, Ogden).
  • "Fresh start": the MEN reported Carrick's words got "a response" [15]. Jackson heard "desperation" [3].
  • Carrick's calm: a weakness (Ogden) against a quality praised in victory and held against him in defeat (Ronay).
  • Baleba coming off: a verdict on his display (Wheeler) against sensible rest after three games in a week (Neville).
  • Conte: an "agreement" (Italian reports) against none (Romano).

Disagree on blame. Ronay, Salt and Whitwell put most of it on the people who built the squad. Carragher puts more on the manager and the players (Attributed) [6][12][21][23].

Unknown.

  • Whether Martínez's thigh keeps him out at Como.
  • Whether Shaw and Mainoo are available for Como or Bournemouth.
  • Whether the back three is a one-off or the new plan.
  • The board's position on Carrick. Ogden says support "won't last long", and the club has said nothing either way.

THE UNITED ROUNDUP VIEW — OPINION

The United Roundup view

Opinion.

I'm with Carrick. For now. And I want to be straight about how much work "for now" is doing in that sentence, because I'm not daft and I watched the same second half as everybody else.

Start with where he started. Amorim was gone two days after the last time United went to Elland Road. Carrick took over on 17 January, and from that day to the end of the season nobody in the league won more points than United. That's third place. It earned him a two-year deal in May. It didn't earn him a squad built for what comes next - a full season plus Champions League.

Because here's what people forget when they talk about last season like we’re automatically going to carry that run of form into this one. Every manager in this league has had a good look at how United played. That's my read, and it shows in the numbers above: more shots per game, fewer of them on target, 49 dribbles completed against us. Casemiro's nine league goals left in the summer without being renewed, and Šeško's twelve are in the treatment room. When the league knows what's coming, you need something different to put in front of them. That means different players, and different players is a summer job.

The summer is where I stop giving anybody an easy ride, and it isn't Carrick. Other clubs outspent United, and how many depends on whose list you read. No striker to back up Šeško. Mbeumo's been used across every position in the front line in successive games. No left-back. Dalot started on Sunday as the fifth different left-back this season - you can coach a lot of things but you can't coach a left-back out of thin air.

Now the fair criticism, because there is some, and he needs to answer it. The second half at Leeds was poor. Within minutes of Martínez going off, Leeds were level, and then United barely had a shot for half an hour. Mount for Baleba looked like swapping like for like when the game wanted something different. Rashford sat on the bench the whole afternoon. On the equaliser, the reports can't agree whose man it was, but my own view is that Mbeumo could have done better to keep his eye on the ball when it dropped to Calvert-Lewin. And our captain is having a rotten time of it: Fernandes was bottom of the ratings again. Carrick's calm can be seen as not a good look or interpreted as being stuck for answers sometimes. When nothing's happening near the dug-out and you're not winning - fans want to know the manager has a plan, especially when everyone watching the game can see that changes are needed. Those are real points.

But he did change things, and he did it in the hardest ground he could have picked. A back three against a side that had gone seven unbeaten at home. Baleba and Santos in midfield, and the pair of them held up. Tielemans moved behind Fernandes, where he's actually useful. For 45 minutes it worked, and Maguire was the best of the lot by every rating. That's a manager trying to solve a problem with what he's been handed. It's not a man who's run out of ideas.

What I won't pretend is that sacking him fixes it. The people who built this squad were sat in the stand at Elland Road, and they're the same people who'd be picking the next fella. If Carrick goes, that's three mid-season changes in a row under the same bosses. At some point you have to ask whether the problem is the man in the dugout or the people choosing him.

So here's where I land. A point at Leeds isn't good enough for Manchester United, and nobody should dress it up as more than it was. But after the week they'd had, United were harder to beat and they scrapped for the shirt. That counts for something.

What would change my mind. Como and Bournemouth. If United lose both, or play the second half from Elland Road for ninety minutes, I'll be the first to say it isn't working. If the players stop running, that's the end of it. And if he gets Shaw and Mainoo back, gets a settled back line, and still can't find a way to win, there's no squad excuse left. Give him those two games and his full-backs. Then let's talk.
